# Why γ∞ = 1/2

The critical exponent γ(D) of the discretely self-similar (DSS) critical solution tends to 1/2 as D → ∞; equivalently
the growth rate of its one unstable mode, λ = 1/γ, tends to 2. This page collects the data, the published arguments, and
an explanation from the structure of the D = ∞ inner problem: there λ = 2 is an exact growth rate, generated by a
symmetry, in the same way that λ = 1 is generated by time translation. The statement and its proof are
[Proposition 5 in the README](README.md#proposition-5-the-unstable-mode-at-infinite-d-is-exact) (and in the paper);
this page has the background and the checks. Notation as in the [README](README.md):
n = D − 2, x = r/(−t), τ = −ln(−t), the inner variable ξ = x/√n, and a perturbation ∝ $`e^{\lambda\tau}`$.

## 1. The data

γ(D) has been computed in double-double precision at 67 points, D = 8.25–61 (`hp/gamma_dd.py`: the root of the
determinant of the exact growth-mode matrix M(λ); `hp/data/gamma_dd.jsonl`, `hp/data/local/gamma_dd.jsonl`). The points
are good to about 10⁻¹² for D = 16–58 and agree with the independent f64 values to 10⁻⁸–10⁻¹². Free fits of λ = 1/γ
in powers of 1/n (`hp/gamma_compare.py`):

| Fit range | 4 powers | 5 powers | 6 powers | 7 powers | 8 powers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D ≥ 24, λ∞ − 2 | 3.8×10⁻⁴ | 1.2×10⁻⁴ | 3.7×10⁻⁵ | 1.1×10⁻⁵ | 3.6×10⁻⁶ |
| D ≥ 30, λ∞ − 2 | 2.4×10⁻⁴ | 6.7×10⁻⁵ | 1.9×10⁻⁵ | 5.5×10⁻⁶ | (1.6 ± 2.2)×10⁻⁶ |

Each added power divides λ∞ − 2 by about 3.5, with no sign of levelling off, and at every order fixing λ∞ = 2 fits
better than leaving it free with the same number of parameters. So λ∞ = 2 to about 10⁻⁶, γ∞ = 1/2 to about 5×10⁻⁷.
With λ∞ = 2 fixed,

```math
\lambda = 2 + \frac{3.0324}{n} - \frac{34.3}{n^2} + \dots, \qquad \gamma = \frac12 - \frac{0.7581}{n} + \dots
```

(the 1/n coefficient is stable to 10⁻⁴ over fit choices: 3.0313–3.0324).

## 2. What the literature says

- **Rozali and Way**, "Gravitating scalars and critical collapse in the large D limit", arXiv:1807.10283 (2018). At
  large D, with rescaled time √D t, they find a family of "oscillaton" solutions labelled by an amplitude ε, whose metric
  function diverges where $`\varepsilon^2 e^{-r^2} = 1`$. Using the divergence radius as a proxy for the black-hole size,
  $`r_{\rm div} = \sqrt{2(\varepsilon - 1)} + O((\varepsilon-1)^{3/2})`$, hence γ = 1/2. They call this suggestive: the
  family is not the DSS critical solution, the divergence is only a proxy for a horizon, and no perturbative analysis or
  1/D correction is attempted. *Against our data:* the value is right. The mechanism, a threshold set by r² rather than r,
  is the one made exact in §4 below.
- **Ecker, Ecker and Grumiller**, "Analytic discrete self-similar solutions of Einstein–Klein–Gordon at large D",
  arXiv:2601.14358 (PRL 2026). They state that "numerical and theoretical arguments suggest lim γ = 1/2, hinting at a CSS
  limit", citing Clark and Pimentel, and leave γ as an open question; no derivation and no 1/D terms.
- **Clark and Pimentel**, "Black hole critical collapse in infinite dimensions: continuous self-similar solutions",
  arXiv:2504.10590, JHEP 11 (2025) 017. Continuously self-similar (CSS) solutions at large D by matched asymptotics; they
  find that the mass critical exponent of self-similarity-breaking perturbations "asymptotes to a constant value in
  infinite dimensions". A summary of the paper gives the exponent as 2(D − 3)/(D − 2); its relation to our radius exponent
  (mass ∝ r^{D−3}) and to the DSS solution was not checked here. It concerns a different (CSS) solution, so it neither
  supports nor contradicts the DSS coefficient 3.0324 directly.
- **Bland, Preston, Becker, Kunstatter and Husain**, CQG 22, 5355 (2005), gr-qc/0507088. γ(D) for 3.5 ≤ D ≤ 14, fitted by
  γ ≈ 0.466 (1 − e^{−0.408D}), i.e. a limit of 0.466. *Excluded:* γ(61) = 0.48935 and the points keep rising.
- **Sorkin and Oren**, hep-th/0502034 (2005). γ(D) up to D = 14, with a suggested maximum near D = 11. *Excluded:* γ is
  monotone up to D = 61 (Bland et al. already saw no maximum).

No published work derives λ∞ = 2 for the DSS solution, or the 1/n coefficient.

## 3. The inner problem is a nonlinear heat equation

At large D the selection of ω and of λ happens beyond all orders in 1/n, in the inner region x ∼ √n
([README, "The inner problem"](README.md#the-inner-problem-at-large-x)), where

```math
L y = c\,\sigma, \qquad L\sigma = c\,\xi y_\xi + c\Big(\frac{y^2}{2} - 1\Big)y, \qquad \xi c_\xi = \frac{c\,y^2}{2}, \qquad L = \xi^2\partial_\xi + \omega\xi\,\partial_\theta .
```

In physical inner variables, ρ = r/√n and the time t, one has ξ = ρ/(−t), so at fixed ρ the operator is a plain time
derivative, $`L = \rho\,\partial_t`$, and $`\xi\partial_\xi = \rho\partial_\rho`$ at fixed t. With

```math
s = \frac{\rho^2}{2} = \frac{r^2}{2n}, \qquad y = \sqrt{s}\,Y, \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{2}\,s\,S,
```

the explicit factors of ρ cancel (`solver/inner_pert/heat_form.py`, sympy) and the inner problem becomes autonomous:

```math
Y_t = c\,S, \qquad S_t = c\Big(Y_s + \frac{Y^3}{4}\Big), \qquad c_s = \frac{c\,Y^2}{4} .
```

This is a nonlinear heat equation, $`Y_s = c^{-1}\partial_t(c^{-1}\partial_t Y) - Y^3/4`$, whose "time" is s = r²/(2n) and
whose "space" is the physical time t. Two known facts fit this picture. The self-similar variable of a heat equation is
t/√s, here −1/ξ, and the linear inner problem is indeed Hermite's (Weber's) equation. The fast mode of the inner problem,
$`e^{-1/(2\xi^2)} = e^{-t^2/(4s)}`$, is the heat kernel.

The autonomous system has these continuous symmetries:

| Symmetry | Generator | Weight under the scaling | Mode of the DSS solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| scaling t → at, s → a²s, Y → Y/a, S → S/a² | self-similarity | 0 | λ = 0 (with the discrete scaling: the θ shift) |
| t-translation | ∂_t | 1 | λ = 1, δZ = (ξ∂_ξ + ω∂_θ)Z (known) |
| s-translation | ∂_s = (1/ρ)∂_ρ | 2 | **λ = 2** |
| time reparametrization t → T(t), c → T′c | gauge | any | removed by the gauge condition |

The t-translation and s-translation are the time and "space" translations of the heat equation. A generator of weight k
acting on the self-similar solution gives a perturbation ∝ $`e^{k\tau}`$, because ∂_t = e^τ(ξ∂_ξ + ω∂_θ) and
∂_s = e^{2τ}ξ^{−2}·ξ∂_ξ at fixed t.

## 4. The λ = 2 mode

Infinitesimally, s → s + ε at fixed Y, S, c gives

```math
\delta y = \frac{e^{2\tau}}{\xi^2}\big(\xi y_\xi - y\big), \qquad \delta\sigma = \frac{e^{2\tau}}{\xi^2}\big(\xi\sigma_\xi - 2\sigma\big), \qquad \delta c = \frac{e^{2\tau}}{\xi^2}\,\xi c_\xi .
```

With the inner series $`y = \sum_j d_j\xi^{2j+1}`$, $`c = \sum_m g_m\xi^{2m}`$ (g₀ = 1), the perturbation series has
$`a_k = 2(k+1)\,d_{k+1}`$ and $`h_m = 2(m+1)\,g_{m+1}`$. It is analytic in ξ, as the matching to the outer 1/n
expansion requires, but it changes c at ξ = 0 by h₀ = 2g₁ = 2F². The value of c there is a lapse normalization (a
function of t), which the matching fixes, h₀ = 0. A time reparametrization t → t + η(t), η = e^τ P(θ), is an exact symmetry
(c → T′c) and adds

```math
\delta y \mathrel{+}= e^{2\tau}P\,Xy, \qquad \delta c \mathrel{+}= e^{2\tau}\big[(P + \omega P')\,c + P\,Xc\big], \qquad X = \xi\partial_\xi + \omega\partial_\theta ,
```

which restores h₀ = 0 when P + ωP′ = −2F² (solvable harmonic by harmonic, since 1 + iνω ≠ 0). The sum is an exact
λ = 2 mode with the gauge fixed. Its leading profile, the coefficient of ξ in δy/2, is

```math
f = (2 + \omega\partial_\theta)(F + \omega F') - 2F^3 + P\,(F + \omega F'), \qquad P + \omega P' = -2F^2 .
```

**Check** (`solver/inner_pert/pert_check3.py`). The perturbation recursion of `pert.py` (gravity included, h₀ = 0 imposed,
nothing about symmetries built in) run at λ = 2 from this f reproduces the predicted a_j and h_j to 10⁻¹⁸⁰ relative in
every harmonic the Galerkin truncation has not yet reached. The background is F = 2.72 sin θ + 0.15 sin 3θ, and the check
runs to j = 11 at M = 45. The front where truncation errors enter moves with M (j ≈ 7 at M = 25, j ≈ 12 at M = 45), as it
should. The same f at λ = 1.9 or 2.1 misses by 7.5% already at j = 3.

## 5. Why its multiplier vanishes, and why it is the physical mode

**The growth-rate condition holds identically at λ = 2.** By the selection rule (Conjecture 1), the D = ∞ critical
solution is the inner solution whose fast-mode (Stokes) multipliers vanish for every harmonic, i.e. whose jump across
the Stokes line is identically zero. A growth rate is a λ at which a real perturbation profile has vanishing multipliers
too (`pert.py`, `pert_ratio.py`). The λ = 2 mode is the derivative of a one-parameter family of exact solutions:
s-translates of the background, composed with time reparametrizations. Every member of that family has a zero jump, so
the perturbation's jump, the derivative of the family's jumps, vanishes too. So λ = 2 is an exact growth rate of the
D = ∞ problem, and no transcendental condition is involved. That is why λ∞ is a simple number while ω∞ is not: ω is
fixed by the vanishing of a multiplier, λ by a symmetry. The statement is as rigorous as Conjecture 1, and needs no
numerics beyond the check above.

**It is physical, not gauge.** The time reparametrization is pure gauge, and the t-translation (λ = 1) is the usual
shift of the accumulation time. The s-translation, though, shifts r², and r is the areal radius, so it is a genuine
deformation. It is a symmetry only of the D = ∞ inner problem: at finite D the outer region and the 1/n corrections to the
inner equations depend on r explicitly. So at finite D it survives as an ordinary unstable mode, with λ = 2 + O(1/n).
This matches the data, λ = 2 + 3.0324/n.

**The physical picture.** In the inner region the collapse is diffusion in s = r²/(2n). The unstable mode moves the
solution along s, so a supercritical evolution forms its horizon at $`s_{\rm BH} \propto (p - p_*)`$, i.e.
$`r_{\rm BH} \propto (p - p_*)^{1/2}`$. This is Rozali and Way's $`r_{\rm div} = \sqrt{2(\varepsilon-1)}`$, now for the DSS
solution and from an exact symmetry.

**The one-harmonic model's 1.765.** There λ = 2 is not a root, for two reasons:
- the model's background is not the D = ∞ solution (its ω is 22% low);
- Galerkin truncation does not commute with the gauge fix, which multiplies by P(θ), so the truncated model has no exact
  λ = 2 mode with h₀ = 0. (The s-translation alone does commute with the truncation.)

## 6. What is not settled

- **Uniqueness.** The argument shows that λ = 2 is a growth rate at D = ∞. It does not show that it is the *only*
  physical unstable mode, i.e. that the DSS solution has no other λ with Re λ > 0 at D = ∞. The finite-D data, one
  unstable mode with λ → 2, are consistent with it.
- **The 1/n coefficient.** λ₁ = 3.0324 should follow from first-order perturbation theory in the symmetry breaking: with
  the O(1/n) corrections to the inner equations (which break the s-translation), $`\lambda_1 = -\,S_{1/n}/\partial_\lambda S\,\big|_{\lambda=2}`$,
  where S(λ) is the perturbation's fast-mode multiplier and $`S_{1/n}`$ the multiplier induced on the λ = 2 mode by the
  O(1/n) terms. This needs the O(1/n) inner problem and ∂_λ S at λ = 2, neither computed here.
- **The eigenvector.** The predicted leading profile f(θ) in §4 can be compared with the unstable eigenvector of the
  double-double solver at large D (from `Solver.mode_matrix`), mapped to the outer leading-order profile. Not done here.
- **Rigor.** The argument inherits the status of Conjecture 1 (gaps (a)–(c) in the README). Given the conjecture, the
  remaining steps are the exact symmetry (§3, symbolic) and the analyticity of the generated perturbation (§4).
