\title[The echo period at large dimension]{The echo period of Choptuik's critical solution at large dimension:\\
a selection rule and a computer-assisted proof}

\author{Nathan Moinvaziri}
\address{Independent researcher}
\email{nathan@nathanm.com}
\date{October 2026; unreviewed draft}

\begin{abstract}
Choptuik's critical solution of spherical scalar-field collapse is discretely self-similar, with an echo period $\Delta$
that depends on the spacetime dimension $D = n + 2$; computed to $D = 61$, it approaches $1.643893746$ as $D \to \infty$, if it is
analytic in $1/D$. We study the
limit as a selection problem of exponential asymptotics. Within a scaling ansatz measured on the solutions, the $1/n$
expansion of the self-similar field equations is unobstructed to all orders for any period and any time profile, so the
period is selected beyond all orders. At leading order the problem is solved by ${}_2F_1$, with a closed-form,
never-vanishing light-cone coefficient; gravity transports the corresponding fast WKB mode by a non-vanishing factor; and
the late orders of the $1/n$ series encode the coefficient as the Stokes multiplier of a mode born at infinity. At
$x \sim \sqrt n$ the equations reduce to an $n$-free inner problem, the leading-order large-$D$ system of Rozali and Way, and we
conjecture that the period is selected by the vanishing of its fast-type Stokes multipliers. In the inner problem a shift
of $r^2$ gives an exact unstable mode with growth rate $2$, which yields the critical exponent $1/2$ under the conjecture
and two further hypotheses. We reduce a selection theorem for models with finitely many harmonics to lemmas, certify its
two contraction arguments for one harmonic, and prove by a computer-assisted argument, assuming Braaksma's summability theorem, that the selection
condition of the one-harmonic model has exactly one zero in an explicit box, and that it is simple. Evaluated numerically
without truncation, the conjectured rule gives $\Dinf = 1.6440 \pm 0.0001$ if the selection function has no other nearby
zeros. The work was done by an AI model (Claude, Anthropic) under the author's direction and has not been reviewed by a human expert.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Choptuik's critical solution~\cite{Choptuik1993} is the spacetime at the threshold of black-hole formation in the
spherically symmetric collapse of a massless scalar field. It is discretely self-similar (DSS): in suitable
coordinates it repeats itself on scales smaller by a fixed factor $e^{\Delta}$, and $\Delta$ is called the echo period.
As a mathematical object it is the solution of a nonlinear eigenvalue problem~\cite{Gundlach1995}: a solution of the
self-similar Einstein--scalar equations that is periodic in logarithmic time, regular at the centre and analytic at the
past light cone of the singularity exists only for isolated values of $\Delta$. In four spacetime dimensions
$\Delta = 3.445452402(3)$~\cite{MartinGarciaGundlach2003}, and Reiterer and Trubowitz proved the existence of the solution
by a computer-assisted argument that encloses $\Delta$ to about $80$ digits~\cite{ReitererTrubowitz2019}. The problem
can be posed in any number $D = n + 2$ of spacetime dimensions, with $n$ entering the equations as a parameter; see the
review~\cite{GundlachReview2025} for the physics.

This paper is about the limit $n \to \infty$. In the companion paper~\cite{companion} the critical solution is
computed up to $D = 61$, and the echo period approaches a finite limit, $\Dinf = 1.643893746$ if $\Delta$ is analytic in $1/D$
(Section~\ref{sec:numbers}).
The questions here are mathematical. Why does the expansion of the equations in powers of $1/n$ not determine $\Dinf$?
What does? And what can be proved about it?

The answer has the structure of a classical selection problem of exponential asymptotics. In Saffman--Taylor finger
selection and in dendritic growth, a formal expansion in a small parameter exists for every value of the quantity to
be selected, and an actual solution exists only when an exponentially small term vanishes; that term is the Stokes
multiplier of an inner equation posed near a complex singular point~\cite{Combescot1986,HongLanger1986,KruskalSegur1991}.
Xie and Tanveer made this rigorous~\cite{XieTanveer2003,TanveerXie2003,Xie2019}. In our problem the small parameter is
$1/n$, the selected quantities are the frequency $\omega = 2\pi/\Delta$ and the time profile of the solution, the
symmetry condition is analyticity at the light cone, and the complex singular point is the point at infinity of the
self-similar radius. Here the unknown depends on the radius and a periodic phase, and the inner problem is an infinite
system coupled through harmonics.

\subsection*{Results}
We work within a scaling ansatz for large $n$ that is measured on the numerical solutions (Ansatz~\ref{ans:scaling}).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The formal $1/n$ expansion exists to all orders for every period and every time profile
(Proposition~\ref{prop:1}), so the period is selected beyond all orders in $1/n$.
\item The leading order is a free wave solved by ${}_2F_1$, with an explicit, never-vanishing light-cone coefficient
$C(n,s)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:2}); gravity transports the corresponding fast WKB mode by a non-vanishing factor
(Proposition~\ref{prop:3}); and the late orders of the $1/n$ series encode $C(n,s)$ as the Stokes multiplier of an
exponential born at $x = \infty$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:4}).
\item At $x \sim \sqrt n$ lives an $n$-free inner problem, which is the leading-order large-$D$ system of Rozali and
Way~\cite{RozaliWay2018} in other variables. Without gravity its Stokes constant is Weber's and reproduces $C(n,s)$. We
conjecture that the $D = \infty$ solution is selected by the vanishing of its fast-type Stokes multipliers
(Conjecture~\ref{conj}), and give the argument, its hypotheses and the three places where rigour fails.
\item In $s = r^2/(2n)$ the inner problem is an autonomous nonlinear heat equation, whose $s$-shift, gauge-fixed, is an
exact perturbation with growth rate $2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:5}); under Conjecture~\ref{conj}, uniqueness of the
unstable mode and continuity in $1/n$, the critical exponent tends to $1/2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:gamma}).
\item A selection theorem for $M$-harmonic models (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}), after Xie and Tanveer, is reduced to lemmas.
The two contraction arguments among them, the global continuation and the inner--outer matching, are proved for each
fixed $M$ and carried out with computer-assisted constants for $M = 1$ and $n \ge e^{73.89}$; what the one-harmonic theorem
still lacks is listed in Section~\ref{sec:B1status}.
\item For the one-harmonic truncation, the selection condition has exactly one zero in an explicit box of half-width
$10^{-9}$, and it is simple (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}). The proof is computer-assisted, assumes only Braaksma's summability
theorem, and follows Baldom\'a, Capi\'nski, Guardia and Seara~\cite{BaldomaCapinskiGuardiaSeara2023}.
\item The untruncated multiplier is numerically an entire function of the squared amplitude, of exponential type; its
first coefficient is enclosed rigorously, by a computer-assisted computation not yet independently reviewed
(Section~\ref{sec:entire}).
\end{enumerate}
Evaluated numerically to twelve orders, the selection rule gives $\Dinf \approx 1.644$ from the $D = \infty$ equations
alone: evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj}, with the fourth digit fixed only under an assumption on the selection function's
other zeros (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).

\subsection*{Prior work}
The leading-order freedom of the period, any periodic profile with any period, was found by Ecker, Ecker and
Grumiller~\cite{EckerEckerGrumiller2026}, who constructed analytic DSS solutions at large $D$ in the same gauge;
Proposition~\ref{prop:1} extends it to all orders (Remark~\ref{rem:eeg} discusses their next-to-leading-order condition). The free wave equation in similarity coordinates was analysed in $3+1$
dimensions by Donninger~\cite{Donninger2009}, which contains the $D = 4$ case of Proposition~\ref{prop:2}; the
coefficient $C(n,s)$ follows from Gauss's connection formula~\cite[Eq.~15.8.4]{DLMF} once the mode equation is recognised
as hypergeometric, and we did not find it for general $D$ in the literature we searched. Proposition~\ref{prop:4} is an
explicit instance of Dingle's late-order analysis~\cite{Dingle1973}, whose nonlinear form is due to Chapman, King and
Adams~\cite{ChapmanKingAdams1998}; the analogous singularity structure for ${}_2F_1$ with large parameters is worked out
by Paris~\cite{Paris2013}. Trans-series corrections in $1/n$ are familiar in large-$D$ gravity~\cite{EmparanHerzog2020}.
Rozali and Way~\cite{RozaliWay2018} derived the $D$-free leading-order equations that are our inner problem, noted that
they are parabolic with the roles of space and time swapped, and estimated $\gamma = 1/2$ from the divergence of their
oscillating solutions. What is new here is their use for the discretely self-similar solution: the inner Stokes
multipliers, the selection conjecture and the symmetry argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:5}. Clark and
Pimentel~\cite{ClarkPimentel2025} constructed continuously self-similar critical solutions at large $D$ by matched
asymptotics. The proof templates are Xie and Tanveer (selection), Baldom\'a, Fontich, Guardia and
Seara~\cite{BaldomaFontichGuardiaSeara} (matching and continuation) and Baldom\'a, Capi\'nski, Guardia and
Seara~\cite{BaldomaCapinskiGuardiaSeara2023} (the computer-assisted Stokes constant). Other rigorous
instances of ``existence if and only if an exponentially small coefficient vanishes'' are in Lombardi's
monograph~\cite{Lombardi2000}.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{The self-similar equations}
Let $D = n + 2$ be the spacetime dimension and $\eps = 1/n$. We use the self-similar radius $x$ (areal radius over time
to the singularity, normalized so that the past light cone of the singularity is at $x = 1$), logarithmic time
$\tau = -\ln(-t)$, and the phase $\theta = \omega\tau$, with $\omega = 2\pi/\Delta$. The unknowns are four functions of
$(x, \theta)$, $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$: $p$ and $m$, the scaled derivatives of the scalar field along the two radial
null directions; $b = a^2$, with $a$ the radial metric function; and $c$, the lapse-type function that sets the light
cones (the past light cone is where $x = c$). With $G = (n-1)\,c\,(b-1)$, the self-similar Einstein--scalar equations
read~\cite{companion}
\begin{align}
x c_x &= (n-1)\,c\,(b-1), \qquad\qquad x b_x = b\,(p^2+m^2) - (n-1)\,b\,(b-1), \label{eq:E12}\\
x(x-c)\,p_x &= -\omega x\,p_\theta + G p - c p + \tfrac{n}{2}\,c\,(p+m), \label{eq:E3}\\
x(x+c)\,m_x &= -\omega x\,m_\theta - G m + c m - \tfrac{n}{2}\,c\,(p+m). \label{eq:E4}
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[the DSS problem]\label{def:dss}
Find $\omega > 0$ and functions $p, m, b, c$ on $0 \le x \le 1$, $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$, with only odd harmonics of
$\theta$ in $p, m$ and only even ones in $b, c$, that solve \eqref{eq:E12}--\eqref{eq:E4} and are
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item regular at the centre $x = 0$,
\item analytic at the past light cone $x = 1$, and
\item normalized by $c(1) = 1$ (the gauge that puts the light cone at $x = 1$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Both $x = 0$ and $x = c = 1$ are regular singular points of \eqref{eq:E3}--\eqref{eq:E4}. Regularity at the centre
removes one branch at $x = 0$; at $x = 1$ the general solution has a branch $(1-x)^{\Lambda}$ with non-integer $\Lambda$,
and requiring its coefficients to vanish is what selects $\omega$, as in~\cite{Gundlach1995}. The centre series has the
exact symmetry $m(x) = p(-x)$.

\subsection{Numerical input}
\label{sec:numbers}
The companion paper~\cite{companion} computes the critical solution at 857 values $8.25 \le D \le 61.03$ in
double-double arithmetic~\cite{Dekker1971}. If $\Delta$ is analytic in $1/D$, which held-out tests there support but do not prove,
$\Dinf = 1.643893746$ and $2\pi/\Dinf = 3.82213590$, to about $10^{-8}$ (an estimator range rather than a calibrated
error; its construction is described there). The growth rate $\lambda$ of the unstable mode, $\lambda = 1/\gamma$
with $\gamma$ the critical exponent of the black-hole radius, $r_{\rm BH} \propto (p - p^*)^{\gamma}$~\cite{KoikeHaraAdachi1995},
tends to $2$: free fits in $1/n$ put $\lambda_\infty = 2$ to about $10^{-6}$. The time profile at the centre,
$F_n(\theta) = \sqrt n\,\partial_x p(0, \theta)$, converges as $D$ grows: its first harmonic extrapolates to about $2.72$,
and $n\,(1 - \langle c(0)\rangle)$ and $\langle F^2\rangle$ approach a common limit near $3.7$ (angle brackets denote the
mean over $\theta$); the measured values are tabulated in~\cite{companion}. None of these numbers is used in a
proof below. Where an exact value of $\winf$ is needed, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, we \emph{define}
$\winf := 2\pi/1.64389375$, the reference value used in all the codes; this is a convention (it differs from
$2\pi/\Dinf$ by $2.4\times10^{-9}$ relative), and the theorem is equally a statement about $\omega$ itself.

\subsection{The scaling at large \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}}
\label{sec:ansatz}
On the double-double solutions~\cite{companion}, $p \approx x F(\theta)/\sqrt n$ near the centre, $p + m$ is smaller
than $p$ by a further factor of order $1/n$, $c = 1 - O(1/n)$ and $b - 1 \approx (p^2+m^2)/(n-1)$. Write $p = \eps^{1/2}P$, $m = \eps^{1/2}M$, $S = P + M$ and $\Delta_P = P - M$. The sum and the difference of
\eqref{eq:E3} and \eqref{eq:E4} are
\begin{align}
x\,c\,\partial_x \Delta_P - c\,\Delta_P &= x^2 \partial_x S + \omega x\,\partial_\theta S - \tfrac{1-\eps}{\eps}\, c\,(b-1)\,\Delta_P ,
\label{eq:sum}\\
S &= \eps\,\frac{x^2 \partial_x \Delta_P - x c\,\partial_x S + \omega x\,\partial_\theta \Delta_P}{c\,(1-\eps)\,b} .
\label{eq:diff}
\end{align}

\begin{ansatz}[scaling]\label{ans:scaling}
The fields have formal expansions in integer powers of $\eps$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ansatz}
S = \sum_{k\ge1}\eps^k S_k,\quad \Delta_P = \sum_{k\ge0}\eps^k D_k,\quad c = 1 - \sum_{k\ge1}\eps^k G_k,\quad
b = 1 + \sum_{k\ge2}\eps^k B_k,\quad \omega = \sum_{k\ge0}\eps^k\omega_k ,
\end{equation}
with coefficients that are functions of $(x, \theta)$, $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$.
\end{ansatz}

The scaling is forced by one measured fact, $c - 1 = O(1/n)$ (gravity is weak: $n(1 - \langle c(0)\rangle)$ converges).
The $b$ equation gives $b - 1 \approx (p^2+m^2)/(n-1)$; the $c$ equation then gives $c - 1 = O(n(b-1)) = O(p^2)$, so
$c - 1 = O(1/n)$ requires $p = O(n^{-1/2})$; and the terms of order $n$ in \eqref{eq:E3}--\eqref{eq:E4} force
$p + m = O(p/n)$, which is the measured $p + m = O(n^{-3/2})$. Integer powers of $\eps$ beyond the overall $\eps^{1/2}$
are an assumption (analyticity in $1/n$), consistent with the held-out tests on $\Delta(D)$ in~\cite{companion} but not
implied by them. All statements of Section~\ref{sec:outer} about the formal expansion hold within this ansatz; whether the
formal family is the asymptotic expansion of actual solutions is hypothesis (H2) of Conjecture~\ref{conj}.

\section{The outer expansion and its late orders}
\label{sec:outer}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$1/n$}{1/n} expansion is unobstructed}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1}
Let $F(\theta)$ be any smooth $2\pi$-periodic function with odd harmonics only, $\omega_0 \neq 0$, $\omega_1, \omega_2,
\dots$ any numbers, and $K_1(\theta), K_2(\theta), \dots$ any smooth odd-harmonic functions. Then
\eqref{eq:sum}--\eqref{eq:diff} together with \eqref{eq:E12} have exactly one formal solution of the form
\eqref{eq:ansatz} with $D_0 = 2xF$ in which, at every order,
\begin{itemize}
\item $S_k$ is an even polynomial in $x$ starting at $x^2$,
\item $D_k - xK_k$ is an odd polynomial starting at $x^3$, and
\item $G_k$ and $B_{k+1}$ are even polynomials ($B$ starting at $x^2$) with $G_k(1) = 0$,
\end{itemize}
all with smooth periodic coefficients. Every order is regular at the centre and analytic at the light cone, and
$m(x) = p(-x)$ holds order by order. (The hypothesis $\omega_0 \ne 0$ only excludes the static case; the proof does not
use it.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $k$, with the structure in the statement as the hypothesis for all orders below $k$. Write $E$ for the
even polynomials in $x$ with smooth periodic coefficients.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{$B_{k+1}$.} The $b$ equation at order $k+1$ gives $B_{k+1}$ algebraically, since its coefficient is
$(n-1)b \approx 1/\eps$, from $x\partial_x B_k$, $(1+B)(P^2+M^2)$ and $(1-\eps)(1+B)B$ at lower orders. Since
$P^2 + M^2 = (S^2 + \Delta_P^2)/2$ with $S \in x^2E$ and $\Delta_P \in xE$, every term lies in $x^2E$.
\item \emph{$G_k$.} The $c$ equation at order $k+1$ reads $-x\,\partial_x G_k = [(1-\eps)\,c\,(b-1)]_{k+1} \in x^2E$.
Dividing by $x$ leaves only odd powers $x^{2j-1}$, $j \ge 1$, so $G_k = -\sum_j r_{2j}x^{2j}/(2j) + \text{const}$ is an
even polynomial without $\log x$. The constant is fixed by $G_k(1) = 0$.
\item \emph{$S_k$.} The numerator $x^2\partial_x\Delta_P - xc\,\partial_x S + \omega x\,\partial_\theta\Delta_P$ of
\eqref{eq:diff} maps $\Delta_P \in xE$, $S \in x^2E$ into $x^2E$. The denominator is $1 + O(\eps)$ with coefficients in
$E$, so its inverse is a power series in $\eps$ with coefficients in $E$. Hence $S_k \in x^2E$.
\item \emph{$D_k$.} Equation \eqref{eq:sum} at order $k$ is $x\,\partial_x D_k - D_k = R_k$. Here $R_k$ collects
$x^2\partial_x S_k + \omega x\,\partial_\theta S_k \in x^3E$, terms $c\,(b-1)\,\Delta_P \in x^3E$, and terms
$G_i\,(x\,\partial_x D_j - D_j)$; the last are in $x^3E$ because $x\partial_x - 1$ annihilates $xK_j$. So $R_k$ is odd and
starts at $x^3$, and
\[
D_k = x\int R_k\,x^{-2}\,\dd x = \sum_{j \ge 1} \frac{R_{k,2j+1}}{2j}\, x^{2j+1} + xK_k .
\]
The only possible obstruction, a term $x^1$ in $R_k$ that would produce $x \log x$ (not allowed at the centre), never
occurs.
\item \emph{Symmetry.} $S$ even and $\Delta_P$ odd in $x$ is the same as $p(-x) = m(x)$.
\item \emph{Harmonic content.} If $P, M$ (hence $S, \Delta_P$) contain only odd harmonics of $\theta$ and $B, G$ only even
ones at the lower orders, every step above preserves this: products of two odd-harmonic factors are even-harmonic,
products of an odd and an even one are odd-harmonic, and $\partial_\theta$ keeps the harmonic.
\end{enumerate}
Polynomials are analytic everywhere, so the light-cone condition holds automatically, and $c(1) = 1$ holds by
construction. Nothing in the construction constrains $F$, $\omega_0$ or the $\omega_k$.
\end{proof}

The leading-order part of this freedom, any periodic profile with any period, is in~\cite{EckerEckerGrumiller2026}.

\begin{remark}[a published next-to-leading-order condition]\label{rem:eeg}
Ecker, Ecker and Grumiller, in the same gauge (their $f = \alpha/a$ is our $c$), require $f \le 1$ near the light cone and
obtain from it, at next-to-leading order, a condition at the zeros of the profile ($\omega^2F'' + 3\omega F' = 0$ there,
in our variables), which leads to their Eq.~(20) and which they take to fix the period~\cite{EckerEckerGrumiller2026}; Lai
and Wen use the same condition to select the period~\cite{LaiWen2026}. It does not constrain the formal solution of
Proposition~\ref{prop:1}. The inequality holds for every regular solution, whatever the period: by \eqref{eq:E12} with
$b(0) = 1$, $b$ cannot fall below $1$, so $xc_x \ge 0$ and $c \le c(1) = 1$. Near a zero of $F$ the next-to-leading-order
term of $b - 1$ is the cross term of a square whose square term arrives one order later, and the computed solutions do
not satisfy the condition~\cite{companion}.
\end{remark}

The first orders (computed symbolically, to fourth order with \repo{solver/largeD\_expand.py 4}) are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstorders}
\begin{gathered}
P_0 = xF,\qquad B_2 = 2x^2F^2,\qquad G_1 = F^2\,(1-x^2),\qquad S_1 = 2x^2\,(F + \omega_0 F'),\\
D_1 = x^3\,(\omega_0^2 F'' + 3\omega_0 F' + 2F - 2F^3) + xK_1 .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Substituting the expansion through $S_3$, $G_3$, $B_4$ into the exact equations, with $F$ from the $D = 60$ solution, the
relative residual of every equation falls like $n^{-3}$ for $n = 50, \dots, 800$, both for $\omega_0 = 2\pi/1.64389$ and
for $\omega_0 = 2\pi/1.4$ (\repo{solver/largeD\_formal\_check.py}).

\begin{remark}[selection beyond all orders]\label{rem:beyond}
If $\Delta(D)$ is analytic in $1/D$, its expansion coefficients cannot be computed order by order from the field
equations: within Ansatz~\ref{ans:scaling} the selection acts through terms smaller than every power of $1/n$. The same
holds for the critical exponent. The expansion of growing perturbations $\propto e^{\lambda\tau}$ of the formal solution
goes through for every $\lambda$, since the induction above works with every $\tau$-derivative of a perturbation replaced
by $\lambda + \omega\partial_\theta$ (checked symbolically through order 2, \repo{solver/inner\_pert/expand\_pert.py}). So
$\lambda = 1/\gamma$ is also selected beyond all orders.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The leading-order problem}
At leading order ($c = b = 1$) the equations are the scalar wave equation on flat space in self-similar form. (In four
dimensions Price and Pullin approximated the critical solution, in each of two regions, by flat-space scalar-field
solutions~\cite{PricePullin1996}.) For one
harmonic $e^{\iu\mu\theta}$ ($\mu$ odd) put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abk}
s = \iu\mu\omega,\qquad \alpha = \tfrac{1+s}{2},\qquad \beta = \tfrac{2+s}{2},\qquad \kappa = \tfrac{n+1}{2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:2}
The solution of the leading-order problem that is regular at the centre is, up to a constant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prop2sol}
p - m = x\,{}_2F_1(\alpha,\beta;\kappa;x^2), \qquad
p + m = \frac{x(1-x^2)\,\partial_x(p-m) - (1+sx^2)\,(p-m)}{x\,(s-n+1)} .
\end{equation}
Near the light cone it contains the non-analytic branch $(1-x^2)^{n/2-1-s}$ with coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
C(n,s) = \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(s+1-\frac n2\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac{2+s}{2}\right)},
\end{equation}
which is finite and non-zero for every real $\omega \ne 0$, odd $\mu$ and $n \ge 2$. Hence no time-periodic self-similar
solution of the linear problem is analytic at the light cone, in any dimension $D \ge 4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $c = b = 1$ the difference equation gives $(n - 1)\,(p+m) = x^2\,\partial_x(p-m) - x\,\partial_x(p+m) + sx\,(p-m)$,
and the sum gives $x\,\partial_x(p-m) - (p-m) = x^2\,\partial_x(p+m) + sx\,(p+m)$. Eliminating $p + m$ gives, for
$y = p - m$,
\[
x^2(1-x^2)\,y'' + x\,\big(n - 2 - 2(s+1)x^2\big)\,y' - \big(n - 2 + s(s+1)x^2\big)\,y = 0 ,
\]
and with $y = x\,w(z)$, $z = x^2$, this is Gauss's equation $z(1-z)w'' + [\kappa - (\alpha+\beta+1)z]\,w' - \alpha\beta\,w = 0$.
Its exponents are $0$ and $1-\kappa$ at $z = 0$, so $y \sim x$ or $y \sim x^{2-n}$; for $n > 2$ regularity, and for $n = 2$
parity ($y = p - m$ is odd in $x$ because $m(x) = p(-x)$, while $x^0$ is even), selects ${}_2F_1(\alpha,\beta;\kappa;z)$.
At $z = 1$ the exponents are $0$ and $\kappa - \alpha - \beta = n/2 - 1 - s$. Since $s$ is imaginary and non-zero,
$\kappa - \alpha - \beta$ is not an integer, and Gauss's connection formula~\cite[Eq.~15.8.4]{DLMF}
\begin{multline*}
{}_2F_1(\alpha,\beta;\kappa;z) = \frac{\Gamma(\kappa)\Gamma(\kappa-\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(\kappa-\alpha)\Gamma(\kappa-\beta)}\,{}_2F_1(\alpha,\beta;\alpha+\beta-\kappa+1;1-z)\\
+ (1-z)^{\kappa-\alpha-\beta}\,\frac{\Gamma(\kappa)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta-\kappa)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\,{}_2F_1(\kappa-\alpha,\kappa-\beta;\kappa-\alpha-\beta+1;1-z)
\end{multline*}
applies. The second coefficient is $C(n,s)$. The Gamma function has no zeros, and $\alpha$, $\beta$ and
$\alpha + \beta - \kappa$ are not poles, because their imaginary parts are $\mu\omega/2$, $\mu\omega/2$ and $\mu\omega$,
all non-zero.
\end{proof}

At $40$ digits the formula satisfies the original first-order equations to $10^{-40}$ for $n = 18$--$1000$. For the
fundamental at $\omega = \winf$, $|C| = 0.0121$, $0.0114$, $0.0124$, $0.0192$, $0.0405$ at $n = 18$, $38$, $58$, $200$,
$1000$: it grows like $\sqrt n$ and is small only through a factor of order $e^{-\pi\mu\omega/2}$. Without gravity a
free wave cannot be both regular at the centre and analytic at the light cone. The branch $(1-x^2)^{n/2-1-s}$ is smaller
than any power of $1/n$ at every fixed $x < 1$, which is why Proposition~\ref{prop:1} never sees it; gravity, through
the $O(1/n)$ couplings, has to cancel its coefficient harmonic by harmonic. The next two subsections show that it cannot
do so inside $0 < x < 1$, and locate where the cancellation must happen instead.

\subsection{What gravity does to the non-analytic piece}
The non-analytic piece is a fast WKB mode: in the exact solution of Proposition~\ref{prop:2} it is
$x^{2-n}(1-x^2)^{n/2-1-s}$, which varies on the scale $1/n$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
\Phi(x) = \ln x - \tfrac12\ln(1-x^2) .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3}
Let $F(\theta)$ and $\omega$ be given, and $c = 1 - \eps F^2(1-x^2) + O(\eps^2)$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:1}. The
perturbations
\[
\delta p = e^{-n\Phi(x)}\big(A(x,\theta) + O(1/n)\big), \qquad
\delta m = e^{-n\Phi(x)}\Big(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\,A(x,\theta) + O(1/n)\Big)
\]
solve the equations linearized about the formal solution on $0 < x < 1$, to the order shown, if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
x(1-x^2)\,\partial_x A - 2\omega x^2\,\partial_\theta A = \big(1 + x - 2x^2F(\theta)^2\big)\,A ,
\end{equation}
that is, along the characteristics $\theta = \theta_0 + \omega\ln(1-x^2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transportsol}
A = A_0(\theta_0)\,\frac{x}{1-x}\,\exp\!\Big(-\int_0^x \frac{2t\,F\big(\theta_0 + \omega\ln(1-t^2)\big)^2}{1-t^2}\,\dd t\Big) .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute into the linearized equations. The terms of order $n$ come from $x(x\mp c)\,\partial_x$ acting on
$e^{-n\Phi}$ and from $\tfrac n2 c\,(\delta p+\delta m)$. With $B$ the amplitude of $\delta m$ they read
\[
-x(x-1)\,\Phi'\,A = \tfrac12 (A+B), \qquad -x(x+1)\,\Phi'\,B = -\tfrac12 (A+B) .
\]
A non-zero solution needs $x(1-x^2)\,\Phi' = 1$, which gives $\Phi$, and then $B = A(1-x)/(1+x)$. At order $1$ the
corrections $(a_1, b_1)$ satisfy a linear system with the same singular matrix,
\[
\frac12\begin{pmatrix} \frac{1-x}{1+x} & -1 \\[2pt] 1 & -\frac{1+x}{1-x} \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ b_1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} R_p \\ R_m \end{pmatrix},
\]
whose left null vector is $(1+x, -(1-x))$. The right-hand sides collect $x(x\mp1)\,\partial_x$ of $A$ and $B$, the
$\omega x\,\partial_\theta$ terms, the $\mp c$ terms, and the gravity terms from $c = 1 - \eps G_1$ inside
$x(x\mp c)\,n\Phi'$ and $\tfrac n2 c$. The perturbations of $b$ and $c$, and $G\,\delta p$, are smaller by $1/n$ and drop
out. Solvability, $(1+x)R_p - (1-x)R_m = 0$, multiplied by $(1+x)/2$, is \eqref{eq:transport}; with
$G_1 = F^2(1-x^2)$ the gravity terms add up to $-2x^2F^2A$. A first-order equation integrates along its characteristics,
and $\int (1+x)/(x(1-x^2))\,\dd x = \ln\big(x/(1-x)\big)$ gives the prefactor.
\end{proof}

Without gravity a harmonic $A = \alpha(x)e^{\iu\mu\theta}$ gives $\alpha = x(1-x)^{-1}(1-x^2)^{-s}$ and
$\delta p - \delta m = 2x^{2-n}(1-x^2)^{n/2-1-s}$: exactly the second solution of Proposition~\ref{prop:2}, with its
exponents at the centre and at the light cone. A symbolic repetition of the derivation from the exact equations, with
$\delta b$ and $\delta c$ kept, confirms that they enter only at order $\eps^{3/2}e^{-n\Phi}$, and direct integration of the
linearized equations reproduces the transported $A$ with relative errors falling like $1/n$ ($0.57$ at $n = 100$ to $0.031$ at
$n = 1600$, for $F = 2.72\sin\theta + 0.22\sin3\theta$, $\omega = \winf$). Three consequences:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Gravity acts on the non-analytic piece at order $1$, not $1/n$ (for the profile above it multiplies it by about $40$
over $0.72 \le x \le 0.9$, the interval of the direct integration). Near the light cone the characteristic winds through all phases and the factor behaves like
$(1-x^2)^{\langle F^2\rangle}$, so the light-cone exponent becomes $n/2 - 1 - s + \langle F^2\rangle$. With
$\langle F^2\rangle \approx 3.7$, an expansion in the amplitude of the wave is not a route to the selection.
\item The factor in \eqref{eq:transportsol} never vanishes, so gravity cannot cancel the fast mode on the way to the
light cone.
\item Every characteristic reaches $x = 0$, where the fast mode is the singular branch $x^{2-n}$. A solution regular at
the centre therefore has $A_0 = 0$ on every characteristic: at any order of this WKB expansion it carries no fast mode on
$0 < x < 1$. The non-analytic piece must come from a Stokes phenomenon.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The light-cone coefficient as a Stokes multiplier}
A divergent series records the exponentially small terms it switches on in its late
orders~\cite{Dingle1973,ChapmanKingAdams1998}: if the switched-on term is $n^{-g}e^{-n\chi(x)}$, the coefficients behave
like $y_k \sim K\,Q(x)\,\Gamma(k-g)/\chi(x)^{k-g}$. At leading order this can be made exact, because the $1/n$ expansion
of $x\,{}_2F_1$ is explicit.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:4}
Let $0 < x < 1$, $\Phi$ as in \eqref{eq:Phi}, $\tau_\ell = \Phi - \iu\pi(\ell + \tfrac12)$ for $\ell \in \mathbb Z$, and
let $y = x\,{}_2F_1(\alpha,\beta;\kappa;x^2)$ with $\alpha, \beta, \kappa$ as in \eqref{eq:abk}, $y \sim \sum_k y_k\eps^k$.
If $|\Phi| < (\sqrt3/2)\pi$ (equivalently $0.066 < x < 0.998$), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prop4}
y_k = K\,x^2(1-x^2)^{-1-s}\,\frac{\Gamma(k+s+\frac12)}{\tau_0^{\,k+s+\frac12}}\,\big(1 + O(1/k)\big), \qquad
K = e^{-\iu\pi(1+s)}\,\frac{2^{-s-1/2}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)} .
\end{equation}
The $\ell = -1$ singularity contributes at the same rate, with a $k$-independent relative weight of $8\times10^{-5}$
($x = 0.5$) to $5\times10^{-8}$ ($x = 0.95$). If $|\Phi| > (\sqrt3/2)\pi$, the late orders are governed instead by the
$x$-independent singularities $\pm\iu\pi$.
\end{proposition}

The proof uses one elementary lemma.

\begin{lemma}[product]\label{lem:product}
Let $A = \sum_k a_k\eps^k$ and $B = \sum_j b_j\eps^j$ be Gevrey-1 series whose Borel transforms have radii of convergence
$\rho_A < \rho_B$, with $a_k$ of exact factorial growth $|a_k| \asymp k!\,\rho_A^{-k}$ (up to powers of $k$). Then
$(AB)_k = b_0a_k\,(1+O(1/k))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$|b_ja_{k-j}/a_k| \lesssim (\rho_A/\rho_B)^j\,j!\,(k-j)!/k!$, and the sum over $j \ge 1$ of the right-hand side is
$O(1/k)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:4}]
\emph{(i) Euler integral.} For $\re\kappa > \re\beta$ (that is, $n > 1$),
${}_2F_1 = \frac{\Gamma(\kappa)}{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\kappa-\beta)}\int_0^1 t^{\beta-1}(1-t)^{\kappa-\beta-1}(1-zt)^{-\alpha}\,\dd t$.
With $1 - t = e^{-2\tau}$ and $\kappa = (1 + 1/\eps)/2$,
\[
y = x\,P(\kappa)\cdot 2\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau/\eps}G(\tau)\,\dd\tau,\qquad
G = e^{(2\beta-1)\tau}(1-e^{-2\tau})^{\beta-1}(1-z+ze^{-2\tau})^{-\alpha},
\]
with $P = \Gamma(\kappa)/(\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\kappa-\beta))$ and $z = x^2$.

\emph{(ii) Singularities of $G$ and Watson's lemma.} Write $G = \tau^{\beta-1}H$. $H$ is analytic except where
$1 - z + ze^{-2\tau} = 0$, i.e.\ at $\tau = \tau_\ell$, each a simple zero, so that $H \propto (1-\tau/\tau_\ell)^{-\alpha}$
there; and at the branch points $\tau = \iu\pi m$, $m \ne 0$, of $(1-e^{-2\tau})^{\beta-1}$. On $\tau > 0$,
$1 - z + ze^{-2\tau} > 1 - z > 0$ and $|G| \le Ce^{\tau}$, so Watson's lemma gives
$2\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau/\eps}G \sim 2\sum_m H_m\Gamma(m+\beta)\eps^{m+\beta}$ with $H = \sum_m H_m\tau^m$.

\emph{(iii) Darboux.} The radius of convergence of $\sum_m H_m\tau^m$ is $R = \min(|\tau_0|, \pi)$. If $|\tau_0| < \pi$,
the only singularities on $|\tau| = R$ are $\tau_0$ and $\tau_{-1}$, both of type $(1-\tau/\tau_\ell)^{-\alpha}$, so
\[
H_m = \sum_{\ell=0,-1} h_\ell\,\frac{\Gamma(m+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,m!}\,\tau_\ell^{-m}\,\big(1+O(1/m)\big),\qquad
h_\ell = \lim_{\tau\to\tau_\ell}H(\tau)\,(1-\tau/\tau_\ell)^{\alpha},
\]
with $H$ continued along the ray from $0$.

\emph{(iv) Prefactor.} $P(\kappa)(2\eps)^{\beta}(1+\eps)^{-\beta}\Gamma(\beta) = 1 + \sum_j p_j\eps^j$, from Binet's
expansion of $\ln\Gamma(\kappa) - \ln\Gamma(\kappa-\beta)$, whose Borel transform is singular only at $\tau = \iu\pi m$;
and $(1+\eps)^{\beta}$ converges.

\emph{(v) Product.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:product}, with $b_0 = 1$ and the powers $\eps^{\pm\beta}$ cancelling,
$y_k = \frac{x\,2^{1-\beta}}{\Gamma(\beta)}\,\Gamma(k+\beta)H_k\,(1+O(1/k))$.

\emph{(vi) Combining.} $\Gamma(k+\alpha)\Gamma(k+\beta)/\Gamma(k+1) = \Gamma(k+s+\tfrac12)(1+O(1/k))$. Evaluating $h_0$ in
closed form, with $e^{2\tau_0} = z/(z-1)$, the factors reduce to $x^2(1-x^2)^{-1-s}$ times $K$, which gives
\eqref{eq:prop4}. If $|\tau_0| > \pi$, then $R = \pi$ and step (iii) applies to $\pm\iu\pi$ instead.
\end{proof}

The prediction is confirmed numerically to order $122$ at $90$ digits (\repo{solver/largeD\_prop4/}): at $x = 0.9$,
$y_k$ divided by \eqref{eq:prop4}, minus one, extrapolates to $(-0.9 - 2.3\iu)\times10^{-3}$ over $k = 100$--$122$; the
measured constant equals $K$ to $1 + 3.65/k$ at $x = 0.5$, $0.8$ and $0.9$; and at $x = 0.999$ and $0.9999$ the measured
singulant is $-3.135\iu \to -\iu\pi$, not $\Phi - \iu\pi/2$, as the statement predicts. The singularities $\pm\iu\pi$ come
from the Gamma-function prefactor and the endpoint factor $(1-e^{-2\tau})^{\beta-1}$; their exponentials $e^{\mp\iu\pi n}$
are pure phases, not exponentially small terms, so they do not enter the Stokes-line argument, which concerns the
$\tau_\ell$.

\begin{remark}[the Stokes line]\label{rem:stokesline}
Put $\chi = \tau_0 = \Phi - \iu\pi/2$. Then $e^{-n\chi} = e^{\iu n\pi/2}x^{-n}(1-x^2)^{n/2}$ is the fast mode of
Proposition~\ref{prop:3}, and $x^2(1-x^2)^{-1-s}$ is its transported amplitude at $F = 0$: the divergence of the $1/n$
series and the non-analytic piece are the same object. On the branch continued from $0 < x < 1$, $\chi$ vanishes only at
$x = \infty$ ($\Phi(\infty) = \iu\pi/2$), so the fast mode is born at the outer singular point of the equations. Its
Stokes line ($\im\chi = 0$, $\re\chi > 0$) is the real axis $x > 1$ approached from above, where
$\chi = \ln\big(x/\sqrt{x^2-1}\big)$. On $0 < x < 1$, $\im\chi = -\pi/2$, so nothing is switched on there, as
consequence (iii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:3} requires. (On the imaginary axis $\im\chi = 0$ too, but there
$\re\chi < 0$.) The Stokes line ends at the light cone, so a loop around $x = 1$ crosses it, and that is where the
branch $(1-x^2)^{n/2-1-s}$ and its coefficient come from: $C(n,s)$ is the Stokes multiplier. Directly, the $1/n$ series
truncated near its smallest term reproduces $x\,{}_2F_1$ inside the light cone far below the size of the singular branch:
at $x = 0.9$, $n = 60$ the smallest difference is $4\times10^{-40}$, against $|C|$ times the branch, $7\times10^{-21}$
(\repo{solver/largeD\_linear\_truncation.py}).
\end{remark}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}-free inner problem and the selection rule}
\label{sec:inner}

\subsection{The inner equations}
Near $x = \infty$ the large-$n$ expansion is not uniform. At $x \sim \sqrt n$ one has $\eps x^2F^2 = O(1)$, so
$c - 1 = O(1)$ and gravity is no longer weak; $p \approx \eps^{1/2}xF = O(1)$; and $e^{-n\Phi} \to e^{\mp\iu n\pi/2}e^{-n/(2x^2)}$,
so fast and slow modes are no longer separated. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:innervars}
\xi = x/\sqrt n,\qquad y = p - m,\qquad \sigma = \sqrt n\,(p+m),\qquad b - 1 = \eta/n,\qquad
L = \xi^2\partial_\xi + \omega\xi\,\partial_\theta .
\end{equation}
At leading order the difference and the sum of \eqref{eq:E3}--\eqref{eq:E4} and the equations \eqref{eq:E12} become
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner}
L\,y = c\,\sigma, \qquad
L\,\sigma = c\,\xi\,\partial_\xi y + c\,\big(\tfrac12 y^2 - 1\big)\,y, \qquad
\xi\,\partial_\xi c = \tfrac12\,c\,y^2 , \qquad \eta = \tfrac12 y^2 .
\end{equation}
(In the difference equation the terms of order $\sqrt n$ are $x^2\partial_x y$, $\omega x\,\partial_\theta y$ and
$nc\,(p+m)$; in the sum the terms of order $1$ are $x^2\partial_x(p+m)$, $xc\,\partial_x y$, $\omega x\,\partial_\theta(p+m)$
and $(G - c)\,y$ with $G = c\eta$; the $b$ equation becomes algebraic, $\eta = p^2 + m^2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:B50} below gives
the corrections exactly.) No large parameter is left: the inner problem is the same for every $n$. Up to a change of
variables it is the leading-order flat-space system of Rozali and Way~\cite[Sec.~2.2]{RozaliWay2018}, in the regime $r =
O(1)$, $t = O(D^{-1/2})$ where they rescale time by $\sqrt D$; the map is written out in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:5}(i) and verified symbolically (\repo{solver/inner\_pert/rw\_map.py}).

The formal solution regular at $\xi = 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:innerseries}
y = \sum_{j\ge0} d_j\,\xi^{2j+1},\qquad \sigma = \sum_{j\ge0} s_j\,\xi^{2j+2},\qquad c = \sum_{j\ge0} g_j\,\xi^{2j},\qquad
d_0 = 2F,\quad g_0 = 1,
\end{equation}
is unique once $F$ is given (the coefficients follow by recursion), and it is Gevrey-1 in $\xi^2$. Its first terms are
$g_1 = F^2$, $s_0 = 2(F + \omega F')$, $d_1 = \omega^2F'' + 3\omega F' + 2F - 2F^3$: the top-degree coefficients of $G_1$,
$S_1$ and $D_1$ in \eqref{eq:firstorders}. In general $d_k$, $-g_k$ and $s_{k-1}$ are the top-degree coefficients of the
outer $D_k$, $G_k$ and $S_k$ (numerically to $2\times10^{-13}$ at orders $1$--$10$, for $F = 2.72\sin\theta$ and
$\omega = \winf$; \repo{solver/largeD\_inner.py}): the inner problem resums the outer series' top-degree terms. The free
functions $K_k$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:1} multiply $x$, the lowest degree, so they never reach the inner problem, which
contains $F$ and $\omega$ only.

\subsection{The linear inner problem}
With $t = 1/\xi$ and the characteristic coordinate $\phi = \theta + \omega\ln t$, one has $L = -\partial_t$ at fixed
$\phi$, and eliminating $\sigma$ from \eqref{eq:inner} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oscillator}
y'' + \Big(c^2t - \frac{c'}{c}\Big)\,y' + c^2\Big[\omega\,\partial_\phi y + \big(1 - \tfrac12 y^2\big)\,y\Big] = 0,
\qquad t\,c' + \omega\,\partial_\phi c = -\tfrac12\,c\,y^2,
\end{equation}
with $' = \partial_t$ at fixed $\phi$: along each characteristic, a softening anharmonic oscillator with friction $c^2t$.
Without gravity ($c = 1$) and for one harmonic, $w = t^{-s}y_\mu$ obeys Hermite's equation $w'' + tw' + (1+s)\,w = 0$, and
$w = e^{-t^2/4}v$ gives Weber's equation $v'' + (\tfrac12 + s - \tfrac14t^2)\,v = 0$. The solution regular at $\xi = 0$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2F0}
y_\mu = 2F_\mu\,\xi\,{}_2F_0(\alpha,\beta;;2\xi^2),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad d_j = 2^j\,\frac{(\alpha)_j(\beta)_j}{j!}\,d_0 ,
\end{equation}
and the second solution carries $e^{-1/(2\xi^2)}$, which is the outer fast mode $e^{-n/(2x^2)}$. The Stokes line of
${}_2F_0(\cdot\,;2\xi^2)$ is the real $\xi$ axis, i.e.\ the real axis $x > 1$. The lateral solutions are
$y^\pm_\mu = 2F_\mu\,\xi\,X^{\alpha}U(\alpha,\tfrac12,X)$, $X = -1/(2\xi^2)$, with $y^+$ on the upper side of the cut of
Kummer's $U$ ($\arg X \to \pi^-$) and $y^-$ on the lower side~\cite[\S\S2.11, 13.7]{DLMF}. Their difference on real
$\xi$ is $2\pi\iu\,K'(s)\,2F_\mu$ times the recessive (fast) solution, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kprime}
K'(s) = \frac{2^{-s-1/2}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Gamma(1+s)}
\end{equation}
by the duplication formula; this normalization is checked in ball arithmetic to $1 \pm 3\times10^{-30}$ at two radii and
two frequencies (\repo{solver/proof/lin\_check.py}). Matching the inner fast mode to the outer one gives the large-$n$
form of the light-cone coefficient of Proposition~\ref{prop:2}, with $\Lambda = n/2 - 1 - s$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Clarge}
C(n,s) = -2\pi\iu\,K'(s)\,(n+1)^{s+1/2}\,e^{-\iu\pi\Lambda}\,\big(1 + O(s^2/n)\big)
= -\iu\,\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\Gamma(1+s)}\,(n+1)^{s+1/2}\,e^{-\iu\pi\Lambda}\,\big(1 + O(s^2/n)\big),
\end{equation}
Weber's classical Stokes constant $\sqrt{2\pi}/\Gamma(-\nu_W)$ with $\nu_W = -1-s$~\cite[\S12.9]{DLMF}. The ratio of the two
sides is $1.00014$ at $n = 10^5$ for $\mu = 1$ ($1.0013$ for $\mu = 3$), with $n\,(\text{ratio} - 1) \to 13.9 - 7.6\iu$, of
the size of $|s^2| = 14.6$ (\repo{solver/largeD\_stokes\_C.py}); the asymptotics need $\mu^2\omega^2 \ll n$. So, for large $n$,
the light-cone coefficient is the $n$-independent Stokes constant $K'(s)$ of a ${}_2F_0$, times $(n+1)^{s+1/2}$ and a phase. In
the linear case the outer late-order constant $K$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:4} and this inner Stokes constant are the same object; this is the exact linear version of the
inner--outer matching.

\subsection{The fast mode with gravity, and the multipliers}
With $\delta y = e^{-1/(2\xi^2)}a$ in the linearization of \eqref{eq:inner}, the terms of order $\xi^{-2}$ cancel and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fastinner}
\xi\,\partial_\xi a + 2\omega\,\partial_\theta a = 2F^2a ,
\end{equation}
which is the large-$x$ limit of \eqref{eq:transport}. Its solutions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label}
a(\xi,\theta) = \xi^{2\langle F^2\rangle}\,e^{\Pi(\theta)}\,g(\theta_0),\qquad \theta_0 = \theta - 2\omega\ln\xi,\qquad
\Pi(\theta) = \frac1\omega\int^\theta\big(F^2 - \langle F^2\rangle\big),
\end{equation}
with $g$ an arbitrary $2\pi$-periodic function of the characteristic label $\theta_0$. Accordingly the jump of the inner
solution across the Stokes line (real $\xi$), the difference of the lateral sums along $\arg\xi^2 = 0^\pm$, has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump}
[y](\xi,\theta) \sim S(\theta_0)\,e^{-1/(2\xi^2)}\,\xi^{2\langle F^2\rangle}\,e^{\Pi(\theta)}\,\big(1 + O(\xi^2)\big),
\qquad \xi\to0^+,\qquad S(\theta_0) = \sum_{\nu\ \rm odd} S_\nu\,e^{\iu\nu\theta_0} .
\end{equation}
In the Borel plane conjugate to $1/\xi^2$ the singularity stays at $\zeta = \tfrac12$ (without gravity the inner series
grows at ratio $d_{j+1}/d_j \sim 2j$), and the Dingle exponents become $g_\nu = -(\tfrac12 + \iu\nu\omega) + \langle F^2\rangle$:
the late orders of label harmonic $\nu$ grow like $\Gamma(j - g_\nu)$, each spread over the harmonics $\nu + 2k$ by
$e^{\Pi}$. Darboux fits with these exponents leave residuals of $5\times10^{-8}$ in the one-harmonic model.

\begin{definition}[fast-type Stokes multipliers]\label{def:mult}
For a model with finitely many harmonics, the formal series \eqref{eq:innerseries} is $1$-summable in every direction
except $\arg\xi^2 = 0$ (Braaksma's theorem~\cite{Braaksma1992}; Lemma~\ref{lem:B4} below). The two lateral sums differ
on $\xi > 0$ by an exponentially small solution of the variational equation, and the coefficients of its first term in
the basis $e^{-1/(2\xi^2)}\xi^{2\langle F^2\rangle}e^{\Pi(\theta)}e^{\iu\nu\theta_0}$ are the multipliers $S_\nu(F,\omega)$.
For the untruncated problem the multipliers are defined order by order in an amplitude expansion
(Section~\ref{sec:amp}); a definition directly in a space of $\theta$-analytic functions is part of gaps (a) and (b)
below.
\end{definition}

The selection conditions are $S_\nu = 0$ for every odd $\nu$: one complex condition per odd harmonic, for the unknowns
$F$'s odd harmonics (less one phase) and $\omega$, so the count balances. Two features make the problem harder than its
scalar precedents. First, gravity couples the fast modes of different harmonics, so the multiplier is a vector indexed
by harmonics with a matrix structure. Second, the linear multipliers grow with the harmonic: $S^{\rm lin}_\nu$ is
proportional to $F_\nu/(\Gamma(\alpha_\nu)\Gamma(\beta_\nu))$, and $1/|\Gamma(\alpha_\nu)\Gamma(\beta_\nu)| \approx 47$ for
$\nu = 1$ and $4.4\times10^6$ for $\nu = 3$, growing like $e^{\pi|\nu|\omega/2}$ with $\pi\winf/2 = 6.004$. So $S(\theta_0)$
formally involves $F$ continued to $\im\theta = \mp\pi\omega/2 \approx \mp6$, while $F$'s harmonics decay only like
$e^{-1.4|\nu|}$, and a harmonic generated only by the nonlinearity already carries a jump $10^2$--$10^4$ times that of
harmonic $1$. Truncating harmonics is therefore not a controlled approximation; Section~\ref{sec:tail} explains why the
weights nevertheless cancel in the tuned problem.

\subsection{The selection rule}

\begin{conjecture}[selection at $D = \infty$]\label{conj}
For $n = D - 2$ let $F_n(\theta) = \sqrt n\,\partial_xp(0,\theta)$ and $\omega_n = 2\pi/\Delta(D)$ be the profile and the
frequency of the critical solution. Assume
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*),leftmargin=3em]
\item $F_n \to F_\infty$ uniformly on a strip $|\im\theta| < \sigma_F$ for some $\sigma_F > 0$, and $\omega_n \to \winf > 0$
(the data give $\sigma_F \approx 1.4$, the harmonic decay rate); and
\item the formal family of Proposition~\ref{prop:1} with data $(F_n, \omega_n, K_k)$ is the asymptotic expansion of the
solution uniformly on $\delta \le x \le 1 - \delta$ (checked: residuals $\propto n^{-3}$ through third order).
\end{enumerate}
Then $S_\nu(F_\infty, \winf) = 0$ for every odd $\nu$, where $S_\nu$ are the fast-type Stokes multipliers of the inner
problem \eqref{eq:inner}. Conversely, a zero of $S$ at which the Jacobian with respect to $(F, \omega)$, modulo the
phase, is invertible should continue to critical solutions for all large $n$.
\end{conjecture}

\subsubsection*{The argument}
The inner problem resums the outer series' top-degree terms; the outer series' light-cone exponential is fixed by the
inner singularity at $x = \infty$ (exactly so in the linear case, where the outer late-order constant of
Proposition~\ref{prop:4} is the inner Weber constant); the switched exponential reaches the light cone through an
invertible map; and analyticity at the light cone forces $C = 0$, hence $S = 0$. The map $S \mapsto C$ is, at leading
order in $1/n$, the composition of three maps:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Inner--outer matching} on $1 \ll x \ll \sqrt n$, where the inner fast mode
$e^{-1/(2\xi^2)}\xi^{2\langle F^2\rangle}e^{\Pi(\theta)}S(\theta_0)$ matches the outer WKB mode $e^{-n\Phi}A$ on the branch above
the real axis $x > 1$ (there $\Phi = \ln(x/\sqrt{x^2-1}) + \iu\pi/2$).
\item \emph{The outer transport} of Proposition~\ref{prop:3} along $x > 1$ with real $\theta$. It has real coefficients
while $\eps x^2F^2 \ll 1$ and acts along real characteristics as multiplication by a non-vanishing function composed with a
shift, so it is invertible on Fourier sequences of at most exponential growth.
\item \emph{The half-monodromy} at the regular singular point $x = 1$. It continues the singular Floquet branch
$(1-x)^{\Lambda}\varphi(\theta - \omega'\ln(1-x))$, $\omega' = \omega/(1 - \langle G(1)\rangle)$, from $x > 1$ to $x < 1$ and
multiplies harmonic $\mu$ by $e^{-\iu\pi\Lambda}e^{\pm\pi\mu\omega'}$: a diagonal map with non-zero entries.
\end{enumerate}
So $C \equiv 0 \Leftrightarrow S \equiv 0$ at leading order. In the linear case this is exact
(Propositions~\ref{prop:2} and~\ref{prop:4} with \eqref{eq:Clarge}).

\subsubsection*{Where rigour fails}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Borel summability.} Borel summability of the nonlinear series is not proved. For a fixed number of harmonics
it is not needed (Section~\ref{sec:layerB}). Recomputed in ball arithmetic, the outer late orders of the one- and
three-harmonic truncations show no Borel singularity near the positive real axis; with three harmonics there is an
$x$-independent one near $\zeta \approx -1.2$, which does not obstruct summation along $\mathbb R^+$ but may approach the
origin as the number of harmonics grows (\repo{proof/B3\_borel.md}, \S5).
\item \emph{Uniformity in the harmonic index.} The weights $e^{\pi|\mu|\omega/2}$ grow like $e^{6.0|\mu|}$ while $F$'s
harmonics decay only like $e^{-1.4|\mu|}$, so an $O(1/n)$ relative error in a high harmonic could be amplified when it
mixes into low harmonics through $e^{\Pi}$. The leading-order equivalence holds harmonic by harmonic, but the $n$ at which
it becomes accurate might grow with the number of harmonics.
\item \emph{Uniformity at $x = 1$.} The outer WKB amplitude is singular there, $A \propto (1-x)^{-1}$, and must be matched
to the Frobenius solution on $1/n \ll |1-x| \ll 1$. The exponents agree, $\Lambda = n/2 - 1 - s + \langle F^2\rangle + O(1/n)$
on both sides, but the matching must be written out.
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:layerB} shows how far each gap is closed for a model with finitely many harmonics: (c) holds for each fixed
number of harmonics up to a routine block-diagonalization, by a Volterra bound that is proved (Lemma~\ref{lem:B6p}); the
inner half of (a) is Braaksma's theorem, and the outer half is replaced by fixed-order truncations, its Borel-sum form
being hypothesis (H-$\sigma$), which the selection theorem does not use; and (b) is narrower than stated here
(Section~\ref{sec:tail}).

\section{An exact unstable mode at infinite dimension}
\label{sec:prop5}

By Remark~\ref{rem:beyond} the growth rate $\lambda = 1/\gamma$ of a perturbation $\propto e^{\lambda\tau}$ is, like $\omega$,
selected beyond all orders in $1/n$, by the same kind of fast-mode multiplier. In the inner problem, however, some growth
rates are exact, because they come from symmetries: $\lambda = 0$ (a shift of $\theta$), $\lambda = 1$ (a shift of the time
of the singularity, $\delta Z = (\xi\partial_\xi + \omega\partial_\theta)Z$ for $Z = (y,\sigma,c)$, which is the $D = \infty$
limit of the gauge mode of the solver), and $\lambda = 2$, the value the numerical growth rates approach. This section
derives the last.

Write the inner problem in physical inner variables: $\rho = r/\sqrt n$, with $r$ the areal radius, and the time $t$.
Then $\xi = \rho/(-t)$, $L = \rho\,\partial_t$ at fixed $\rho$, and $\xi\partial_\xi = \rho\partial_\rho$ at fixed $t$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heatvars}
s = \frac{\rho^2}{2} = \frac{r^2}{2n},\qquad y = \sqrt s\,Y,\qquad \sigma = \sqrt2\,s\,W
\end{equation}
(this $s$ is unrelated to the $s = \iu\mu\omega$ of \eqref{eq:abk}).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:5}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item In the variables \eqref{eq:heatvars} the inner equations \eqref{eq:inner} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat}
Y_t = c\,W, \qquad W_t = c\,\Big(Y_s + \frac{Y^3}{4}\Big), \qquad c_s = \frac{c\,Y^2}{4},
\end{equation}
that is, the nonlinear heat equation $Y_s = c^{-1}\partial_t(c^{-1}\partial_tY) - Y^3/4$ in which $s$ plays the role of time
and $t$ that of space. The system contains no explicit $s$ or $t$, so it is invariant under $s$-translation,
$t$-translation, the scaling $(t, s, Y, W) \mapsto (at, a^2s, Y/a, W/a^2)$ and the reparametrizations $t \mapsto T(t)$,
$c \mapsto T'c$.
\item Let $(y, \sigma, c)(\xi, \theta)$ be a discretely self-similar solution of \eqref{eq:inner} with frequency $\omega$,
regular at $\xi = 0$, with $y = 2F\xi + O(\xi^3)$ and $c = 1 + F^2\xi^2 + O(\xi^4)$. Let $P(\theta)$ be the periodic
solution of $P + \omega P' = -2F^2$, and $X = \xi\partial_\xi + \omega\partial_\theta$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode2}
\delta y = e^{2\tau}\Big(\frac{\xi y_\xi - y}{\xi^2} + P\,Xy\Big), \quad
\delta\sigma = e^{2\tau}\Big(\frac{\xi \sigma_\xi - 2\sigma}{\xi^2} + P\,X\sigma\Big), \quad
\delta c = e^{2\tau}\Big(\frac{c_\xi}{\xi} + (P + \omega P')\,c + P\,Xc\Big)
\end{equation}
solves the inner equations linearized about $(y, \sigma, c)$: it is an exact perturbation with growth rate $\lambda = 2$.
It is regular at $\xi = 0$ ($\delta y$ odd and $\delta c$ even in $\xi$), it keeps $c = 1$ at the centre ($\delta c = 0$ at
$\xi = 0$), and its leading profile is $\delta y = 2f\xi e^{2\tau} + O(\xi^3)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f}
f = (2 + \omega\partial_\theta)(F + \omega F') - 2F^3 + P\,(F + \omega F') .
\end{equation}
\item Wherever the fast-type Stokes multipliers of the background are defined (by Borel summation; gap (a)), the
multipliers of this perturbation are the image of the background's under a linear map, and they vanish when those
vanish. So if the $D = \infty$ critical solution is selected by Conjecture~\ref{conj}, $\lambda = 2$ is one of its exact
growth rates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) With $\rho\partial_\rho = 2s\partial_s$ the explicit factors of $\rho$ cancel; this is a direct substitution, done
symbolically with zero residual (\repo{solver/inner\_pert/heat\_form.py}). No coefficient depends on $s$ or $t$, which
gives the translations. Under the scaling every term of each equation picks up the same power of $a$. Under
$t \mapsto T(t)$, $Y$ and $W$ are scalars and $\partial_t$ picks up $T'$, which $c \mapsto T'c$ absorbs.

\emph{The same system as Rozali and Way's.} Their metric is $-(1-\delta)A\,\dd t^2 + \dd r^2/A + r^2\dd\Omega^2$, with
$\tau_{\rm RW} = \sqrt D\,t$, $A = 1 - A_0/D$ and $\delta = \delta_0$ at leading order. Their leading-order equations
$\partial_r\delta_0 = -2r(\partial_{\tau_{\rm RW}}\phi_0)^2$ and
$\partial_r\phi_0 = r(1-\delta_0)^{-1/2}\partial_{\tau_{\rm RW}}[(1-\delta_0)^{-1/2}\partial_{\tau_{\rm RW}}\phi_0]$ are posed at $r = O(1)$ and
times of order $D^{-1/2}$, which is the inner region. With their $(r, \tau_{\rm RW})$ identified with our $(\rho, t)$ and
their $1 - \delta_0$ with our $c^2$, they read $\phi_s = c^{-1}\partial_t(c^{-1}\phi_t)$ and $c_s = \phi_t^2/c$. Then
$Y = 2\phi_t/c$ and $W = Y_t/c$ satisfy \eqref{eq:heat} identically (\repo{solver/inner\_pert/rw\_map.py}); the coefficient
$1/4$ of the cubic term is not adjusted but comes out of the map.

(ii) A generator of weight $k$ under the scaling, applied to a self-similar solution, gives a perturbation
$\propto e^{k\tau}$. With $\tau = -\ln(-t)$, $\partial_t$ at fixed $\rho$ is $e^{\tau}(\partial_\tau + \xi\partial_\xi)$,
which is $e^\tau X$ on a DSS background, and $\partial_s = \rho^{-1}\partial_\rho = e^{2\tau}\xi^{-2}\,\xi\partial_\xi$ at fixed
$t$. So $s$-translation, $(\delta Y, \delta W, \delta c) = (Y_s, W_s, c_s)$, has weight $2$ and gives the first terms of
\eqref{eq:mode2}; for instance $\sqrt{s}\,\partial_s(y/\sqrt{s}) = \rho^{-2}(\rho y_\rho - y)$. At $\xi = 0$ it changes $c$
by $\lim c_\xi/\xi = 2F^2$ per unit of $e^{2\tau}$, which the normalization $c(t, 0) = 1$ forbids. The infinitesimal
reparametrization $t \mapsto t - \eta(t)$ with $\eta = e^{\tau}P(\theta)$ moves the scalars by
$\eta\,\partial_t = e^{2\tau}P X$ and moves $c$ by that plus $\eta' c = e^{2\tau}(P + \omega P')\,c$. At $\xi = 0$, where
$Xc = 0$, the two changes of $c$ cancel exactly when $P + \omega P' = -2F^2$. Harmonic by harmonic this reads
$(1 + \iu\nu\omega)P_\nu = -2(F^2)_\nu$, and $1 + \iu\nu\omega \ne 0$, so $P$ exists, is unique and is as smooth as $F^2$. A
symmetry maps solutions to solutions, so its generator solves the linearized equations. For regularity, write
$y = \sum_j d_j\xi^{2j+1}$ and $c = \sum_m g_m\xi^{2m}$: the $s$-translation part is
$\sum_{k\ge0} 2(k+1)\,d_{k+1}\,\xi^{2k+1}$ for $y$ and $\sum_{m\ge0} 2(m+1)\,g_{m+1}\,\xi^{2m}$ for $c$, and $X$ preserves parity in
$\xi$. The leading coefficient is $2d_1 + 2P(F + \omega F')$, with $d_1 = \omega^2F'' + 3\omega F' + 2F - 2F^3$, which is
\eqref{eq:f}.

(iii) Write the perturbation as $\delta Z = \mathcal D Z$, where $\mathcal D$ is a first-order differential operator in
$(\xi, \theta)$ whose coefficients ($\xi^{-2}$, $\xi^{-1}$, $P(\theta)$, $P'(\theta)$ and constants, times $e^{2\tau}$) are
analytic away from $\xi = 0$. The formal solution regular at $\xi = 0$ with a given leading profile and $\delta c(0) = 0$
is unique (the same recursion as for the background), so the formal series of $\delta Z$ is $\mathcal D$ applied to the
background's. Lateral Borel--Laplace sums commute with differentiation and with multiplication by convergent series, so
the jump of $\delta Z$ across the Stokes line is $\mathcal D$ applied to the background's jump, which is zero when the
background's multipliers vanish.
\end{proof}

The perturbation recursion of the repository (gravity included, $\delta c(0) = 0$ imposed, nothing about symmetries built
in), run at $\lambda = 2$ from the profile \eqref{eq:f}, reproduces the predicted coefficients to $10^{-180}$ relative in
every harmonic the Galerkin truncation resolves, for $F = 2.72\sin\theta + 0.15\sin3\theta$, through $j = 11$ at $45$
harmonics (\repo{solver/inner\_pert/pert\_check3.py 45 11}); with $\lambda = 1.9$ or $2.1$ the same $f$ misses by $7.5\%$ at
the third order. The modes $\lambda = 0$ and $\lambda = 1$ pass the same kind of check, to $6\times10^{-30}$ and (with
gravity) $10^{-177}$. The inner fast mode $e^{-1/(2\xi^2)} = e^{-t^2/(4s)}$ is the heat kernel.

The mode is physical, not gauge: $s$-translation shifts $r^2$, with $r$ the areal radius, which gives
$r_{\rm BH}^2 \propto p - p^*$, the discretely self-similar counterpart of Rozali and Way's estimate
$\gamma = 1/2$~\cite{RozaliWay2018}; the physical reading is in~\cite{companion}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gamma}
Suppose that Conjecture~\ref{conj} holds, that the $D = \infty$ critical solution has no unstable mode other than
$\lambda = 2$ and the gauge mode $\lambda = 1$, and that $\lambda(D)$ is continuous at $1/n = 0$. Then
$\gamma_\infty = 1/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:5}(iii) and Conjecture~\ref{conj}, $\lambda = 2$ is a growth rate of the $D = \infty$ critical
solution, and by the second hypothesis it is its only physical unstable mode. By continuity the growth rate $\lambda(D)$
of the unstable mode tends to $2$, and $\gamma = 1/\lambda \to 1/2$.
\end{proof}

At finite $D$ the outer region and the $1/n$ corrections to the inner equations depend on $r$ explicitly
(Lemma~\ref{lem:B50}), so the symmetry is broken and the mode survives as an ordinary unstable mode with
$\lambda = 2 + O(1/n)$, whose $1/n$ coefficient is measured in~\cite{companion}. Two questions are open: whether
$\lambda = 2$ is the only physical unstable mode at $D = \infty$, and that coefficient, which by first-order perturbation
theory is
$-S_{1/n}/\partial_\lambda S$ at $\lambda = 2$, with $S$ the perturbation's fast-mode multiplier and $S_{1/n}$ the part induced
by the $O(1/n)$ inner equations. The one-harmonic model of Section~\ref{sec:thmA} has a root $\lambda = 1.765$; this is not a
counterexample, because its background is not the $D = \infty$ solution and because Galerkin truncation does not commute
with the gauge step, which multiplies by $P(\theta)$.

\section{The selection theorem programme}
\label{sec:layerB}

This section sets out a proof of the selection rule for models with finitely many harmonics, after Xie and
Tanveer~\cite{XieTanveer2003,Xie2019}, with inner--outer matching after Baldom\'a, Fontich, Guardia and
Seara~\cite{BaldomaFontichGuardiaSeara}. It states what is proved, what is cited, what is certified for one harmonic, and
what remains. The details are in the repository's \repo{proof/C\_selection.md}, \repo{proof/B3\_borel.md},
\repo{proof/B5\_inner.md} and \repo{proof/B8\_constants.md}; uniformity in the number of harmonics is
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:tail}. Throughout this section $z = \xi^2 = x^2/n$.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$M$}{M}-harmonic model and the theorem}

\begin{definition}\label{def:Mmodel}
For odd $M$ let $P_M$ keep the harmonics $e^{\iu\mu\theta}$ with odd $|\mu| \le M$ for $p, m$ and even $|\mu| \le M + 1$ for
$b, c$. The \emph{$M$-harmonic model} is \eqref{eq:E12}--\eqref{eq:E4} with $P_M$ applied to every right-hand side. For
real fields it is a finite analytic system of ODEs in $x$, with $4N + 2(2N+1) = 8N + 2$ real unknowns, $N = (M+1)/2$.
\end{definition}

The even harmonics go up to $M + 1$, not $M - 1$, because that is the truncation of the inner problem solved in
Section~\ref{sec:thmA} and by the inner solvers: with $|\mu| \le M - 1$ the inner limit of the model would be a different
problem ($c$ on $\{0\}$ instead of $\{0, \pm2\}$ for $M = 1$). The certificates of Lemmas~B5 and B8 below project every binary
product onto the harmonics of its result; for $M = 1$ this is the same as projecting each right-hand side once, for
$M \ge 3$ it is not.

The solvers of~\cite{companion} are not such truncations: the double-precision solver works on a Fourier grid in $\tau$
with a cut-off, and the double-double solver uses collocation in $\tau$ with no spectral truncation of the products. Both
discretize the full problem, which the $M$-harmonic models approximate as $M$ grows, so the $M$-harmonic model is a model
of the computed solutions, not the discrete system actually solved. $P_M$ commutes with $\partial_\theta$ and
preserves the parity classes, which is all that the induction of Proposition~\ref{prop:1} uses.

\begin{thmnamed}{B($M$)}\label{thm:B}
Fix odd $M$ and let $(F_*, \omega_*)$ be a zero of the inner multipliers, $S_\mu(F_*, \omega_*) = 0$ for all odd
$\mu \le M$, at which the Jacobian of $(\re S_\mu, \im S_\mu)$ with respect to ($F$'s harmonics, $\omega$), with the phase of
$F_1$ fixed, is invertible. Assume Lemmas~B3($M$), B5($M$) and B6($M$) below in $C^1$ form, and the global continuation
Lemma~B8($M$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Characterization.} For $n \ge n_0$, the centre-regular solution with data $(F_n, \omega)$ near
$(F_*, \omega_*)$ is analytic at the light cone exactly when, for all odd $\mu \le M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cnmu}
C_{n,\mu}(F_n, \omega) = \kappa_{n,\mu}\,\big[S_\mu(F_n^{(0)}, \omega) + R_{n,\mu}(F_n, \omega)\big] = 0 ,
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{n,\mu} \ne 0$ is explicit, $R_{n,\mu} = O(1/n)$ in $C^1$ uniformly near $(F_*, \omega_*)$, and
$F_n^{(0)}$ is the leading part of the profile (the corrections $K_k$ enter only through $R$). In the linear case
$\kappa_{n,\mu} \propto -2\pi\iu\,(n+1)^{s+1/2}e^{-\iu\pi\Lambda_\mu}$, $s = \iu\mu\omega$, as in \eqref{eq:Clarge}, times the
transport and matching factors.
\item \emph{Existence and uniqueness of the branch.} There is a unique solution $(F_n, \omega_n)$ of these equations near
$(F_*, \omega_*)$, with $F_n - F_* = O(1/n)$ and $\omega_n - \omega_* = O(1/n)$. It gives a discretely self-similar solution
of the $M$-harmonic model, regular at the centre and analytic at the light cone.
\item \emph{Converse.} If a sequence of such solutions has $(F_n, \omega_n) \to (F_\infty, \omega_\infty)$, then
$S(F_\infty, \omega_\infty) = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thmnamed}

\begin{proof}[Proof given the lemmas]
The first equality in \eqref{eq:Cnmu} is the chain: the light-cone coefficient equals $k_{n,\mu}$ times the coefficient of
the fast mode just inside $x = 1$ (Lemma~B6); this equals $k_{n,\mu}$ times the transport factor times the fast-mode content
generated on $x > 1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:3} and the half-monodromy); that content is the outer Stokes jump on the line
$x > 1$ (Lemmas~B3 and B8); and the outer jump is the matching factor times $[S_\mu + O(1/n)]$ (Lemma~B5 with Lemma~B4).
Every factor is non-zero and explicit at leading order, and the $O(1/n)$ is uniform in $C^1$. Divide by
$\kappa_{n,\mu}$. The map $Z \mapsto S(Z) + R_n(Z)$ on a neighbourhood of $Z_* = (F_*, \omega_*)$ in $\mathbb R^{2N}$ is
$C^1$-close to $S$, which has an invertible derivative at $Z_*$, so the finite-dimensional implicit function theorem (or a
quantitative inverse-function theorem, since $\|R_n\|_{C^1} = O(1/n)$) gives a unique zero $Z_n$ with
$|Z_n - Z_*| = O(1/n)$. The converse follows by continuity: $S(Z_n) = -R_n(Z_n) = O(1/n) \to 0$.
\end{proof}

The correction is $O(1/n)$, not $O(n^{-1/2})$, because the inner variable is $\xi^2 = x^2/n$ and the outer equations depend
on $n$ only through $n$ and $x^2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:B50}). The non-degeneracy hypothesis is what layer A certifies
(Section~\ref{sec:thmA} for $M = 1$); at $M = 5$ the reduced $2\times2$ Jacobian has singular values about
$6.8\times10^3$ and $3.9\times10^3$ numerically.

\subsection{The proved lemmas}

\begin{lemnamed}{B1($M$)}\label{lem:B1}
For every smooth $F$ with odd harmonics $|\mu| \le M$, every $\omega_0$ and every choice of the free data ($\omega_k$ and
$K_k$, also truncated), the $M$-harmonic model has exactly one formal solution with the structure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:1}.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
Repeat the induction of Proposition~\ref{prop:1} with $P_M$ applied to each right-hand side. Steps (1)--(5) use only
algebraic operations and $\partial_\theta$, integration in $x$, and the parity of $x$-polynomials; $P_M$ acts on the
$\theta$-dependence only, commutes with all of these, and maps even (odd) $x$-polynomials with periodic coefficients to
the same class. Step (6) holds because $P_M$ preserves the odd and even harmonic classes.
\end{proof}

Write the system as $Y_x = n\,\mathcal F_1(x, Y) + \mathcal F_0(x, Y, \partial_\theta Y)$ with $Y = (p, m, b, c)$ and
$\mathcal F_1$ collecting the terms of order $n$. The slow manifold of the formal solution is $b = 1 + O(\eps^2)$,
$p + m = O(\eps^{3/2})$.

\begin{lemnamed}{B2($M$)}\label{lem:B2}
On the slow manifold, the Jacobian of $\mathcal F_1$ with respect to $(p, m, b, c)$ has, for each harmonic, the eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_u = \frac{c^2}{x\,(x^2-c^2)}, \qquad \lambda_b = -\frac1x, \qquad 0, \qquad 0 .
\]
The $\lambda_u$ eigenvector is the combination $u = p + m$ (with a $b$-component), the $\lambda_b$ eigenvector is $b - 1$,
and $v = p - m$ and $c$ are slow. At $c = 1$, $\lambda_u = -\Phi'(x)$, the singulant of Proposition~\ref{prop:3}.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
The terms of order $n$ contain no $\theta$-derivative, so at leading order they act diagonally on harmonics. Read the
four right-hand sides off the equations as functions of $(p, m, b, c)$, take the Jacobian, divide by $n$, let
$n \to \infty$ and set $b = 1$, $m = -p$. The result is the list above (computed symbolically,
\repo{solver/proof/fast\_eigenvalues.py}).
\end{proof}

Consequently there are no turning points on the real axis; $x = 0$ and $x = c(1) = 1$ are doubly singular points of the
type $\eps\,(x - x_0)\,y' = f$; and $x = \infty$ is a turning point, because $\lambda_u \sim n/x^3$ and $\lambda_b = -n/x$ fall to the
size $\sim 1/x$ of the slow terms at $x \sim \sqrt n$. The inner problem is the turning-point equation of this singular
perturbation problem, as in Xie and Tanveer's finger and Kruskal and Segur's dendrite. The fast block is $\lambda_u$ times the
identity on the $N$ odd harmonics at leading order; the transport and the gravity coupling lift the degeneracy at order $1$.

\begin{lemnamed}{B5.0}\label{lem:B50}
In the inner variables $x = \sqrt n\,\xi$, $y = p - m$, $\sigma = \sqrt n\,(p+m)$, $b = 1 + \eta/n$, with $\nu = n^{-1/2}$ and
the $p - m$ equation multiplied by $\nu$, the exact equations \eqref{eq:E12}--\eqref{eq:E4} become
\[
\mathcal I(Z) + \nu^2\,\mathcal Q_1(Z) + \nu^4\,\mathcal Q_2(Z) = 0, \qquad Z = (y, \sigma, \eta, c),
\]
where $\mathcal I$ is the inner problem and $\mathcal Q_1$, $\mathcal Q_2$ are polynomials in $Z$ and its first derivatives; for
instance the $c$ equation is $\xi c_\xi - c\eta + \nu^2c\eta = 0$ and the $p + m$ equation is
$\xi^2\sigma_\xi + \omega\xi\sigma_\theta - c\,\xi y_\xi + c\,(1 - \eta)\,y + \nu^2c\eta y = 0$. Only even powers of $\nu$ occur, and the same
holds for the $M$-harmonic model.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution, done symbolically (\repo{solver/proof/inner\_corrections.py}); projection onto $|\mu| \le M$ commutes
with every step.
\end{proof}

With $\eta = \tfrac12 y^2$ the leading equations are \eqref{eq:inner}. The $\eta$ equation is singularly perturbed (its
$\nu^2$ term contains $\xi\eta_\xi$): this is the $b$ fast mode $\eta \sim x^{-n}$, which is exponentially small in every
direction for $|x| > 1$, so $\eta$ is slaved to $y$, $\eta = \tfrac12 y^2 + n^{-1}\eta_1 + O(n^{-2})$, and takes part in no
Stokes phenomenon. A solution of the inner problem therefore solves the exact equations up to $n^{-1}\mathcal Q_1 + O(n^{-2})$,
and the multipliers receive corrections in integer powers of $1/n$, the first from the linearized inner problem forced
by $\mathcal Q_1$.

For $|x| > 1$ put $w(x) = -\tfrac12\ln(1 - x^{-2})$.

\begin{lemnamed}{B5.1}\label{lem:B51}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Continuing $\Phi$ from $0 < x < 1$ to $x > 1$ through the upper or the lower half plane gives $\Phi = w \pm \iu\pi/2$, so
$e^{-n\Phi} = e^{\mp\iu n\pi/2}e^{-nw}$.
\item With $x^2 = nz$, $nw = \frac1{2z} + \frac1{4nz^2} + \frac1{6n^2z^3} + \dots$, so $e^{-nw} = e^{-1/(2z)}\big(1 + O(1/(n|z|^2))\big)$.
\item The fast mode of Proposition~\ref{prop:3} contributes to $y$ and $\sigma$ the factors $-2x^2/(x^2-1) = -2(1 + \frac1{nz} + \dots)$
and $-2\sqrt n\,x/(x^2-1) = -2z^{-1/2}(1 + \frac1{nz} + \dots)$.
\item Along the characteristic the label continues as
$\theta_0 = \theta - \omega\ln(1 - x^2) = \theta - 2\omega\ln\xi - \omega\ln n \pm \iu\pi\omega + O(\frac1{nz})$, and the gravity factor
contains $(1 - x^2)^{\langle F^2\rangle} = e^{\mp\iu\pi\langle F^2\rangle}n^{\langle F^2\rangle}z^{\langle F^2\rangle}(1 - \frac{\langle F^2\rangle}{nz} + \dots)$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence, in inner variables, the outer fast mode with label harmonic $\mu$, in the gauge $\tilde A_0 = A_0e^{-\Pi}$, is the inner
fast mode $e^{-1/(2z)}z^{\langle F^2\rangle}e^{\Pi(\theta)}e^{\iu\mu\theta_0^{\rm in}}$ times
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
\kappa^{\pm}_{n,\mu} = -2\,e^{\mp\iu n\pi/2}\,e^{\mp\iu\pi\langle F^2\rangle}\,n^{\langle F^2\rangle - \iu\mu\omega}\,e^{\mp\pi\mu\omega}\,
\Big(1 + O\big(\tfrac1{n|z|^2}\big)\Big),
\end{equation}
which is explicit and non-zero. In the $M$-harmonic model the scalar $n^{\langle F^2\rangle - \iu\mu\omega}$ becomes the invertible
matrix $n^{\mathcal G_M}$, with $\mathcal G_M$ the truncated transport generator. At $F = 0$ the factor is $n^{-s}$ times the
$\sigma$-normalization $n^{1/2}$ and the half-monodromy phase: the large-$n$ form $(n+1)^{s+1/2}$ of the linear $C(n,s)$.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
(1) For $x > 1$, $1 - x^2 = (x^2-1)e^{\mp\iu\pi}$ on the two continuations, so $-\tfrac12\ln(1-x^2) = -\tfrac12\ln(x^2-1) \pm \iu\pi/2$
and $\Phi = -\tfrac12\ln(1 - x^{-2}) \pm \iu\pi/2$. (2) $nw = -\tfrac n2\ln\big(1 - \tfrac1{nz}\big) = \tfrac n2\sum_{k\ge1}(nz)^{-k}/k$.
(3) and (4) follow from $\delta m/\delta p = (1-x)/(1+x)$, from \eqref{eq:transportsol}, and from the same continuation as in
(1); the expansions are printed by \repo{solver/proof/inner\_corrections.py}. Collecting the factors gives
\eqref{eq:kappa}.
\end{proof}

The relative correction in (2) is $O(1/(n|z|^2))$, small only for $|z| \gg n^{-1/2}$, i.e.\ $|x| \gg n^{1/4}$; this sets the
outer edge of the matching domain of Lemma~B5 below.

\medskip
\emph{The global geometry.} Fix $\delta \in (0, \tfrac14)$, an angle $\vartheta_0 \in (0, \pi/4)$ and $k_1 > \tfrac12$, and put
$\alpha_B = \pi/4 + \vartheta_0/2$ and $R_n = (k_1 n/\ln n)^{1/2}$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dn}
\mathcal D^+_n = \big\{x : 0 < |x| \le R_n,\ 0 \le \arg x \le \alpha_B\big\} \setminus \big\{|x - 1| < \delta/2\big\}, \qquad
\mathcal D^-_n = \overline{\mathcal D^+_n}.
\end{equation}
$\mathcal D^+_n$ contains the segment $(0, 1 - \delta/2]$, an arc around the light cone through the upper half plane, the
Stokes line $[1 + \delta/2, R_n]$, and, in $z = x^2/n$, the directions $\arg z \in (\pi/2, \pi/2 + \vartheta_0]$ near
$|z| = k_1/\ln n$, where $e^{-1/(2z)}$ is exponentially large. It avoids $x = \pm1$ and the point $x = c(x) \approx n/F^2$
beyond $R_n$.

\begin{lemnamed}{B8.1 (progressive paths)}\label{lem:B81}
For $x \in \mathcal D^+_n$ let $P_x$ be the path from $0$ along the ray $\arg x = \alpha_B$ to radius $|x|$, followed by the arc of
radius $|x|$ clockwise to $\arg x$. Then $P_x \subset \mathcal D^+_n$, and $\re\Phi$ is non-decreasing along $P_x$; so is the
real part of the $b$-mode phase $\ln x$.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
Write $r = |x|$, $\varphi = \arg x$ and $Q = |1 - x^2|^2 = 1 - 2r^2\cos 2\varphi + r^4$, so that $\re\Phi = \ln r - \tfrac14\ln Q$.
On the ray $\varphi = \alpha_B$, $\partial_r\re\Phi = (1 - r^2\cos 2\alpha_B)/(rQ) > 0$, since $\cos 2\alpha_B < 0$ (this is where
$\alpha_B > \pi/4$ is used). On the arc, $\partial_\varphi\re\Phi = -r^2\sin 2\varphi/Q < 0$ for $0 < \varphi < \pi/2$, so $\re\Phi$
increases as $\varphi$ decreases from $\alpha_B$. Since $|re^{\iu\varphi} - 1|$ increases with $\varphi \in [0,\pi]$, the arc
from $\alpha_B$ down to $\arg x$ stays outside the disc $|x - 1| < \delta/2$ whenever its endpoint $x$ does. Finally
$\re\ln x = \ln r$ is constant on the arc and increasing on the ray.
\end{proof}

So every fast Volterra kernel $e^{-n(\Phi(x) - \Phi(x'))}$, with $x'$ before $x$ on $P_x$, has modulus at most $1$; the
transport factor contributes at most $|x/x'|^{2\re\langle F^2\rangle}$.

\begin{lemnamed}{B8.2 (Stokes geometry)}\label{lem:B82}
On $\mathcal D^+_n$ the anti-Stokes curve $\re\Phi = 0$ runs from $x = 1/\sqrt2$ to $\infty e^{\iu\pi/4}$, and the only Stokes
line is $x > 1$, where $\chi = \Phi \mp \iu\pi/2 = w$ is real and positive. Hence the continuation of the centre-regular
solution through the upper half plane carries no free fast mode on $\mathcal D^+_n$: it has none near the centre, where
$e^{-n\Phi}$ is exponentially large, and no Stokes line is crossed before $x > 1$ is reached from above. Its light-cone
singularity is the monodromy of that continuation around $x = 1$, and the difference $Y^+ - Y^-$ of the two continuations
on $x > 1$ is the Stokes jump; by the half-monodromy it equals $C_n(e^{-\iu\pi\Lambda} - e^{\iu\pi\Lambda})\phi$, with $\phi$ the
Frobenius branch of Lemma~B6.
\end{lemnamed}

The light cone is handled by a Volterra bound. Near $x = 1$, with $t = 1 - x$, the transport of Proposition~\ref{prop:3}
becomes $tA_t = \mathcal T A$ with $\mathcal T = -\omega\partial_\theta - 1 + F(\theta)^2$. Here $F^2$ couples the harmonics at
order $1$, but the gauge $e^{\Pi}$ removes the coupling exactly: $(-\omega\partial_\theta + F^2)\psi = \lambda\psi$ has the
periodic solutions $\psi = e^{\Pi(\theta)}e^{\iu\mu\theta}$ with $\lambda = \langle F^2\rangle - \iu\mu\omega$. So, untruncated, the
light-cone exponents are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda}
\Lambda_\mu = \tfrac n2 - 1 - \iu\mu\omega + \langle F^2\rangle + O(1/n),
\end{equation}
and they differ pairwise by the non-zero imaginary amounts $\iu(\nu-\mu)\omega$: no two differ by an integer, for any $n$.
In the $M$-harmonic model $\mathcal T$ is replaced by its truncation $\mathcal T_0 = P_M\mathcal TP_M$, which breaks the gauge step; at
the $M = 5$ zero of Section~\ref{sec:trunc} and for $M$ up to $25$, the central eigenvalues converge to
$\langle F^2\rangle - 1 - \iu\mu\omega$ exponentially in $M$, and all pairwise differences have $|\re| < 0.02$ and
$|\im| \ge 6.89$ ($|\im| \ge 7.26$ for $M \ge 3$) (\repo{solver/proof/lightcone\_exponents.py}).

\begin{lemnamed}{B6$'$ (Volterra bound)}\label{lem:B6p}
Let $t_0 = \tfrac12$. Split the linearized $M$-harmonic system on $t \in (0, t_0]$ into fast components $a$ (the $p + m$
direction of Lemma~B2, one per odd harmonic) and slow components $v$ ($p - m$, $b - 1$ and $c$, after eliminating $b - 1$
along its own fast direction), so that
\[
t\,a' = \big(n\,\ell(t)\,I + \mathcal T_0 + t\,\mathcal T_1(t)\big)\,a + t\,\mathcal Q(t)\,v, \qquad v' = \mathcal R(t)\,a + \mathcal S(t)\,v ,
\]
with $\ell(t) = t\,\Phi'(1-t) = 1/((1-t)(2-t))$, $\mathcal T_0 + t\mathcal T_1$ the order-$1$ transport, and $\mathcal Q, \mathcal R, \mathcal S$
analytic and bounded uniformly in $n$. Let $U$ solve $t\,U' = (\mathcal T_0 + t\mathcal T_1)\,U$ exactly and put
$\psi(t) = \int^t \ell(t')\,\dd t'/t'$. Then for every $\mathbf k \in \mathbb C^N$ there is a solution
\[
a = e^{n\psi(t)}\,U(t)\,\big(\mathbf k + r(t)\big), \qquad v = e^{n\psi(t)}\,O(t/n), \qquad \sup_{0 < t \le t_0}|r(t)| \le C|\mathbf k|/n ,
\]
with $C$ depending only on $M$, $F$, $\omega$ and $t_0$.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
(1) $\psi' = \ell/t > 0$ and $e^{n\psi} \sim t^{n/2} \to 0$ as $t \to 0$. (2) Take for $v$ the particular solution
$v = \int_0^t(\cdots)$, which is fast-type; then $v' = \mathcal Sv + \mathcal R\,e^{n\psi}U(\mathbf k+r)$, and one integration by parts in the
factor $e^{n\psi}$ gives $v = e^{n\psi}\big[t\,\mathcal RU(\mathbf k+r)/(n\ell) + O(t/n^2)\big]$, using $\ell \ne 0$; the boundary term at
$t = 0$ vanishes. (3) Substituting the ansatz for $a$, the transport cancels exactly and
$r' = e^{-n\psi}U^{-1}\mathcal Qv = t\,U^{-1}\mathcal Q\mathcal R U\,(\mathbf k+r)/(n\ell) + O(t/n^2)$. With $U(t) = t^{\mathcal T_0}(I + O(t))$, the
entries of $U^{-1}(\mathcal Q\mathcal R)U$ are $O(t^{-\rho})$, where $\rho$ is the largest difference of the real parts of the
eigenvalues of $\mathcal T_0$: $\rho = 0$ untruncated and $\rho < 0.02$ in the truncations. So
$|r'| \le C_2\,t^{1-\rho}(|\mathbf k| + |r|)/n$, which is integrable at $0$. (4) With $r(t_0) = 0$, Gronwall's inequality gives
$|r(t)| \le |\mathbf k|\,\big(e^{C_2 t_0^{2-\rho}/n} - 1\big) \le C|\mathbf k|/n$ uniformly up to $t = 0$.
\end{proof}

As $t \to 0$, $a \sim t^{n\ell(0)}t^{\mathcal T_0}(\mathbf k + r(0))$, the Frobenius branch with $\Lambda_\mu = n/2 + $ (eigenvalues of
$\mathcal T_0$). This gives:

\begin{lemnamed}{B6($M$) (light-cone matching)}\label{lem:B6}
The exact solution of the linearized $M$-harmonic system that agrees at $t_0$ with the WKB fast mode of label harmonic $\mu$
equals $k_{n,\mu}\phi_\mu$ plus a solution analytic at $x = 1$, where $\phi_\mu = (1-x)^{\Lambda_\mu}(e_\mu + O(1-x))$ is the unique
(by non-resonance) singular Frobenius branch, and $k_{n,\mu} = k^{(0)}_\mu(1 + O(1/n))$ with $k^{(0)}_\mu \ne 0$ explicit from the
transport. In the linear case the identification is exact (Propositions~\ref{prop:2}--\ref{prop:3}).
\end{lemnamed}

What remains in Lemma~B6($M$) is the routine block-diagonalization of the order-$n$ part on $[t_0, 1 - \delta]$ by an
analytic transformation bounded uniformly in $n$, which exists because the fast eigenvalue there is simple and
non-vanishing (Lemma~B2). Because $x - c$ has a simple zero at the light cone, the WKB phase and the Frobenius exponent are
the same object, and the argument is Liouville--Green error control up to a regular singular point~\cite[Chs.~10--12]{Olver1974};
for second-order linear equations the existence and Borel summability of exact WKB solutions is in~\cite{Nikolaev2023}.

\subsection{Lemmas B3 and B4}

\begin{lemnamed}{B3($M$)}\label{lem:B3}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item For every odd $M$, every $(F, \omega)$ and every $\rho > 0$, the formal solution of Lemma~B1 is Gevrey-1 uniformly on
$|x| \le \rho$, in the gauge $c(0) = 1$ and in the light-cone gauge: there are $C, t_1 > 0$ with $|Y_k(x)| \le C\,k!\,t_1^{-k}$
there, so its Borel transform in $\eps$ is analytic on $|\zeta| < t_1$ for every such $x$.
\item For $n \ge n_0$ the centre-regular solution with profile $F_n$ exists on $(0, 1 - \delta/2]$ and differs from the
truncated formal series by the fixed-order remainder of Lemma~B8.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemnamed}

Part (i) is proved by a majorant argument closed by the implicit function theorem for a positive polynomial system
(\repo{proof/B3\_borel.md}, Lemma~B3.7). Part (ii) is Lemma~B8 restricted to the real segment. The results we read on
singularly perturbed systems, with a turning point~\cite{CanalisDurand1993} or doubly singular
points~\cite{CanalisDurandMozoSchaefke2007}, assume every component fast, while here $p - m$ and $c$ are slow, so neither
part is a citation.

For the centre-regular solution to be the $1$-sum of the outer series in $\eps$ along $\arg\eps = 0$, uniformly on compacts
of $(0, 1]$, the Borel transform must continue, with exponential bounds, to a neighbourhood
$S_\sigma = \{\zeta : \operatorname{dist}(\zeta, [0, \infty)) < \sigma\}$ of $[0, \infty)$; by Nevanlinna's theorem in Sokal's
form~\cite{Sokal1980} that is exactly the condition. We call it hypothesis (H-$\sigma$). Theorem~\ref{thm:B} does not use it:
Lemmas~B5 and B8 work with actual solutions and fixed-order truncations. The condition is on the distance from $[0, \infty)$,
not on the argument: the $u$-mode singulant $\Phi(x) - \iu\pi/2$ has argument down to $-0.30\pi$ at $x = 0.95$, but it lies on
$\im\zeta = -\pi/2$. (H-$\sigma$) is proved in the linear case with $\sigma = \pi/2$, and near $\zeta = 0$ for every $M$ by
part (i), which excludes the would-be singularity at $2\Phi(x)$ near $x = 1/\sqrt2$, where $2\Phi$ reaches $0$. Elsewhere it is
supported by a ball-arithmetic computation of the late orders with Pad\'e approximants~\cite{Pade1892} (\repo{proof/B3\_borel.md}, \S5).

\begin{lemnamed}{B4($M$)}\label{lem:B4}
For fixed $M$, $F$ and $\omega$, the inner problem \eqref{eq:inner}, written for the harmonic components of
$y = \xi\,\mathsf Y(z)$, $\sigma = \xi^2\,\mathsf\Sigma(z)$, $c = \mathsf C(z)$, is a finite system $z^2\,\dd\mathsf Z/\dd z = H(z, \mathsf Z)$ with $H$
polynomial, $z = 0$ being an irregular singular point of Poincar\'e rank $1$. Its formal power-series solution with
$\mathsf Y(0) = 2F$, $\mathsf C(0) = 1$ is $1$-summable in every direction except $\arg z = 0$~\cite{Braaksma1992}, its only
exponential being $e^{-1/(2z)}$. The multipliers $S_\nu(F, \omega)$ of Definition~\ref{def:mult} are therefore well defined.
\end{lemnamed}

The first equation is $2z\mathsf Y_z + (1 + \iu\mu\omega)\mathsf Y = (\mathsf C\mathsf\Sigma)_\mu$, regular-singular; the $c$ equation
$\mathsf C_z = \tfrac14(\mathsf C\mathsf Y^2)$ is regular; and eliminating $\mathsf Y_z$ from the second equation leaves
$2z^2\mathsf\Sigma_z = (\mathsf C^2\mathsf\Sigma)_\mu + O(z)$. Braaksma's theorem needs only a meromorphic system and a formal
power-series solution. Costin's sharper rank-one theorems~\cite{Costin1998} do not apply as stated, because the fast
eigenvalue is repeated across harmonics and the slow directions have eigenvalue $0$; they are not needed, because only
the formal power series, not a general transseries, is summed. The repeated eigenvalue makes the first exponential term
a vector over harmonics: its $N$ complex coefficients are the $S_\nu$, the count the selection needs. Braaksma's theorem is
stated for $x^{\nu+1}y' = F(x, y)$ without parameters, and the abstract of Ramis and Sibuya's proof~\cite{RamisSibuya1994}
does not address them either. The analytic dependence of the sums, and of the $S_\nu$, on $(F, \omega)$, which
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} uses, follows from uniqueness on a ray where $e^{-1/(2z)}$ is exponentially large and a contraction there
that is uniform in the parameters; the Picard iterates are analytic in $(F, \omega)$ and converge uniformly
(\repo{proof/B3\_borel.md}, Lemma~B4.P). For $M = 1$ this contraction is the one certified on a parameter box in
Section~\ref{sec:thmA}.

\subsection{The two contractions}
Fix $\tfrac14 < \varpi < \tfrac12$, with $\vartheta_0$ and $k_1$ as above, and let
\[
\mathcal M^+ = \big\{z : n^{-1+2\varpi} \le |z| \le k_1/\ln n,\ 0 \le \arg z \le \pi/2 + \vartheta_0\big\}, \qquad \mathcal M^- = \overline{\mathcal M^+},
\]
which is $n^{\varpi} \le |x| \le R_n$, $0 \le \arg x \le \alpha_B$, a subset of $\mathcal D^+_n$. On $\mathcal M^\pm$ the corrections of
Lemmas~B5.0 and B5.1 have relative size at most $n^{1-4\varpi} \to 0$. By Braaksma's theorem the inner formal solution
has two lateral sums, $Z^+_{\rm in}$ analytic and asymptotic to the series on $-\pi/2 < \arg z < 5\pi/2$ and $Z^-_{\rm in}$ on
$-5\pi/2 < \arg z < \pi/2$; $Z^+_{\rm in}$ is characterized by being asymptotic to the series where $e^{-1/(2z)}$ is
exponentially large, $\pi/2 < \arg z \le \pi/2 + \vartheta_0$. Differences are measured in the variables
$(P, \Phi, C, \vartheta)$ defined by $y = \xi P$, $\Phi = 2zP_z$, $C = c$, $\eta = z\Theta$, $\vartheta = \Theta - \tfrac12[PP]$, with
weights $m_\Phi(z) = |z|$ and $m_P = m_C = m_\vartheta = 1$. The working domain $\mathcal W^+_n \supset \mathcal M^+$ is
$n^\varpi \le |x| \le (k_1n/(\cos\vartheta_0\ln n))^{1/2}$, $0 \le \arg x \le \alpha_B$.

\smallskip
\emph{Hypothesis} (H8) (the input from Lemma~B8). $Y^+_{\rm out}$ is analytic, and analytic in the parameters, on a
neighbourhood of $\mathcal W^+_n$; and on the inner arc $|x| = n^\varpi$, $0 \le \arg x \le \alpha_B$, the difference
$W = Y^+_{\rm out} - Z^+_{\rm in}$ satisfies, for every harmonic, $|W_P| \le \delta_P/n$, $|W_\Phi| \le \delta_\Phi n^{-2+2\varpi}$,
$|W_C| \le \delta_C/n$ and $|W_\vartheta| \le \delta_\vartheta/n$. At the upper end of the arc this is the quantitative form of
``no exponentially large fast content''.

\begin{lemnamed}{B5($M$)}\label{lem:B5}
Let $Y^\pm_{\rm out}$ be the continuations of the centre-regular solution through the upper and lower half planes, written in
inner variables.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Sectorial identification.} Assume (H8). Then on $\mathcal W^\pm_n \supset \mathcal M^\pm$,
$|W_j(z)| \le x_j\,m_j(z)/n$ for every component $j$, where $x$ is any positive solution of an explicit linear inequality
$b + Lx < x$ (componentwise), with $b$ the forcing plus the data $\delta$ of (H8). Such an $x$ exists for $n \ge n_0(M)$.
\item \emph{The jump.} On $\arg z = 0$, $Y^+_{\rm out} - Y^-_{\rm out}$ is a fast-type solution of the variational equation whose
label function has $\mu$-th coefficient
$J_{n,\mu} = \kappa_{n,\mu}\big[S_\mu(F,\omega) + O\big(n^{-1+1/(2k_1)}(\ln n)^{\re\langle F^2\rangle}\big)\big]$, uniformly in $C^1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemnamed}

Part (1) is proved by a bootstrap rather than a contraction mapping, because the $b$-mode needs paths along which $|z|$
does not decrease while the fast mode needs progressive ones (ray $\arg z = \pi/2 + \vartheta_0$, then an arc); the data come
from (H8) on the whole inner arc, no exponentially small information is used, and the constants in $L$ and $b$ are suprema
of explicit expressions in the inner solution (\repo{proof/B5\_inner.md}, \S\S2--4). For $M = 1$, with
$\varpi = \vartheta_0 = 0.3$ and $k_1 = 0.6$, every constant is certified in ball arithmetic over a complex parameter box
containing the box of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, and for data $\delta \le (1, 200, 10, 20)$ the fixed point holds for $n \ge e^{73.89}$ with
$x = (392, 9.2\times10^4, 21, 30)$ (computer-assisted, \repo{solver/proof/b5\_bounds.py}). The threshold is set by the
radius $|z| \approx 0.009$ on which a perturbative fixed point around the inner series closes: the outer edge $k_1/\ln n$
of $\mathcal M^\pm$ must lie inside it.

Part (2) is proved given part (1). Both jumps are fast-type: $\Delta_{\rm in} = Z^+_{\rm in} - Z^-_{\rm in} =
e^{-1/(2z)}z^{\langle F^2\rangle}e^{\Pi}k_{\rm in}(\theta_0)(1 + O(z))$ with $k_{\rm in} = S$, and $\Delta_{\rm out}$ has the same form up to
the factor \eqref{eq:kappa}. At the evaluation point $z_* = k_1/\ln n$ on $\arg z = 0$, $|e^{-1/(2z_*)}| = n^{-1/(2k_1)}$, small
only polynomially, so
\[
k_{\rm out} - k_{\rm in} = \frac{(W^+ - W^-)(z_*)}{e^{-1/(2z_*)}z_*^{\langle F^2\rangle}} + k\cdot O\Big(\frac{(\ln n)^2}{n}\Big)
= O\big(n^{-1+1/(2k_1)}(\ln n)^{\re\langle F^2\rangle}\big)
\]
by part (1), which is $o(1)$ since $k_1 > \tfrac12$; translating to outer label harmonics multiplies by $\kappa_{n,\mu}$.
Replacing $Z_{\rm in}$ by $Z_{\rm in} + n^{-1}Z^{(1)}$, with $Z^{(1)}$ the first inner correction forced by $\mathcal Q_1$, would give
the bracket $S_\mu + n^{-1}S^{(1)}_\mu$ plus a smaller error, i.e.\ the rate $O(1/n)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}; part (1) has not been
run in that form.

\begin{lemnamed}{B8($M$) (global continuation)}\label{lem:B8}
For each $K_0 \ge K_*$ and $n \ge n_0$ the centre-regular solution continues analytically to $\mathcal D^\pm_n$, and there
\[
Y^\pm(x) = \sum_{k \le K_0}\eps^k\,Y_k(x) + R^\pm(x), \qquad |R^\pm(x)| \le C_{K_0}\,(1 + |x|)\Big(\frac{(1+|x|)^2}{n}\Big)^{K_0+1},
\]
with $Y_k$ the polynomial coefficients of Proposition~\ref{prop:1}, of degree at most $2k+1$. For $n \ge n_0$ it supplies
hypothesis (H8) of Lemma~B5(1).
\end{lemnamed}

On $\mathcal D^\pm_n$, $(1+|x|)^2/n \le 2k_1/\ln n$, so $Y^\pm$ has no exponentially large fast content, as Lemma~B5(1) needs. The
proof (\repo{proof/B8\_constants.md}) is a bootstrap in weighted norms of this form: the fast components ($p + m$ per harmonic,
and $b - 1$) are integrated along the paths of Lemma~B8.1, gaining $1/(n|\Phi'(x)||x|) \sim |x|^2/n \le k_1/\ln n$ at large
$|x|$ and $O(1/n)$ on bounded sets; the slow components are integrated from $x = 0$, where regularity fixes them; the
transport factor is absorbed in the fast-kernel bound, so it puts no condition on $K_0$; and the light-cone disc is never
entered. It is proved for every fixed $M$, without a numerical $n_0(M)$. For $M = 1$ the lemma holds with explicit constants
for $n \ge e^{62}$ ($K_0 = 4$, on the domain with $R_n$ replaced by $(k_1n/(\cos\vartheta_0\ln n))^{1/2}$, in the gauge $c(0)$
equal to its formal value), and it supplies (H8) for $n \ge e^{73.89}$ with $\delta = (8.3\times10^{-5}, 79.04, 3.232, 6.464)$
(computer-assisted, in ball arithmetic). No Gevrey bound uniform up to $|x| \sim R_n$ is needed, because the exponentially
small information enters only at the evaluation point $z_*$.

For $M = 1$ the model is that of Definition~\ref{def:Mmodel}, which is also the model of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, and every link

\subsection{Gap (b): the harmonic tail}
\label{sec:tail}
Every constant above depends on $M$. The selection theorem for the full problem needs them uniform in $M$, or a
formulation in a space of functions analytic in a $\theta$-strip, as in Costin and Tanveer's Borel summability for
PDEs~\cite{CostinTanveer2007}. The repository's analysis of this gap (\repo{proof/B\_tail.md}) finds it real but not fatal,
and narrower than its statement in Section~\ref{sec:inner}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The weights cancel in the tuned problem.} Stated without harmonics, the condition is $S \equiv 0$ on the real
label circle, and it involves $F$ only on the real line. The weights $e^{\pi|\nu|\omega/2}$ are the linear connection
coefficients of each label harmonic: they multiply zero in $S \equiv 0$, and they cancel in each tuning equation, because the
free and the forced parts of $S_\nu$ are jumps of the same mode, so that $f_\nu = -(\text{forced jump})/(2\times\text{linear
coefficient})$ is an $O(1)$ functional of the lower harmonics. In the amplitude expansion of Section~\ref{sec:amp} the
leading tuning ratio $q_\nu = f_{\nu+2}/f_\nu$ is independent of $\nu$ to six digits, $q_\infty = 0.008524 + 0.000808\iu$
($|q|A^2 = 0.0633$), with no trace of the factor $e^{2\cdot6.004} = 1.6\times10^5$; the tuned profile decays by $0.0574$ per pair
of harmonics, i.e.\ it is analytic on $|\im\theta| < \sigma_F = 1.428$; and the neighbour-family amplitudes in the late orders
fall by $10^4$--$10^8$ per pair of harmonics instead of growing by about $25$.
\item \emph{Naive truncation brings the weights back.} Projecting the amplitude recursion onto $|\mu| \le M$ changes $\sigma_M$
by about $5\times10^{-4}$, as order counting predicts, but gives $\sigma_{M+1}$ too large by a factor $10^4$--$10^6$ ($-399$ at
$M = 3$, $-1371$ at $M = 5$). A tail lemma must therefore be stated for the \emph{jump-conditioned} truncation ($S_3 = \dots = S_M = 0$
imposed and the higher labels eliminated by a Schur complement), whose sequence converges (Section~\ref{sec:trunc}), not for a
Galerkin projection of the equations.
\item \emph{The tail size looks provable.} The jump-conditioned sequence alternates and shrinks by a ratio of $-0.045$ to
$-0.057$ per pair, the profile's own decay times a coupling of $0.7$--$1.0$. That puts the tail above $M = 5$ at about $4$--$6$
in the harmonic-1 residual $r_1$, above $M = 7$ at $0.2$--$0.35$, and above $M = 9$ at about $0.015$; a certified box of radius
$10^{-3}$ needs the tail below about $4$, so with $M = 7$ explicit the margin is about $13$. Even a proven ratio of $0.25$ per
pair would suffice.
\item \emph{A framework.} For $0 < \varsigma < \sigma_F$ let $X_\varsigma$ be the odd trigonometric series with
$\|F\|_\varsigma = \sum_\nu|F_\nu|e^{\varsigma|\nu|} < \infty$, an algebra of functions analytic on $|\im\theta| < \varsigma$, with
the multipliers measured in the dual weights. At leading order the tuning map is the weighted shift $f_{\nu+2} = q_\infty A^2f_\nu$,
of norm $0.0633\,e^{2\varsigma}$ on $X_\varsigma$, which is below $1$ for $\varsigma < 1.380$ (e.g.\ $0.47$ at $\varsigma = 1$). The target
is then: \emph{in $X_\varsigma$ with $\varsigma \approx 1$, $\|(I - P_M)F\|_\varsigma \le C_1(|q_\infty|A^2e^{2\varsigma})^{(M+1)/2}$, and the
residual of the jump-conditioned $M$-harmonic problem differs from the untruncated one by at most
$L\,\|(I - P_M)F\|_\varsigma$, with explicit $C_1, L$ such that the bound at $M = 7$ is below about $2$.} The first half is a
contraction estimate; the second, with $L$ estimated for the tuned system, is the genuinely new part.
\item \emph{Secondary Stokes lines.} The singularities of $F$ at $\im\theta = \pm1.428$ reach complex $\xi$ along
$\arg\xi \to \mp0.374$, between the Stokes and anti-Stokes lines. They are absent at each finite order in the amplitude (every
order is a trigonometric polynomial); where they sit at $|\xi| = O(1)$ has not been computed, so a proof should define $S_1$
order by order in $A^2$ and sum in $A^2$ last.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theorem A(\texorpdfstring{$M=1$}{M=1}): a computer-assisted proof}
\label{sec:thmA}

This section proves that the selection condition of the one-harmonic truncation of the inner problem has exactly one
zero in an explicit box, and that it is simple. The model is a test case, not the physical problem: its zero lies
$22\%$ below $\winf$ in frequency and does not survive the addition of harmonic $3$ (Section~\ref{sec:trunc}). Its role is to
show that the inner multipliers can be certified, along a route that extends to more harmonics. The details are in
\repo{proof/A\_certify.md} \S\S4--12 and the independent review \repo{proof/A\_review.md}; the code is in
\repo{solver/proof/}. Notation in this section is local.

\subsection{The one-harmonic problem}
Let $\bx = (A, \omega/\winf)$ with $\winf := 2\pi/1.64389375$ (a convention, Section~\ref{sec:numbers}), and
$F = A\sin\theta$, so $F_{\pm1} = \mp\iu A/2$. The $M = 1$ truncation of \eqref{eq:inner} keeps $y$ and $\sigma$ on the harmonics
$\mu = \pm1$ and $c$ on $\nu = 0, \pm2$, and projects every product onto its result's harmonics, written $[\cdot]_\mu$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M1}
\xi^2 y_\mu' = [c\sigma]_\mu - \iu\mu\omega\xi\,y_\mu,\qquad
\xi^2 \sigma_\mu' = [c\,\xi y']_\mu + [c\,e\,y]_\mu - [c\,y]_\mu - \iu\mu\omega\xi\,\sigma_\mu,\qquad
\xi\,c_\nu' = [c\,e]_\nu,
\end{equation}
with $e = \tfrac12[yy]$ and $' = \dd/\dd\xi$. This is a system for seven complex unknowns, polynomial in them and rational in
$\xi$, with an irregular singular point at $\xi = 0$. Its formal solution
$y_\mu = \sum_j d_{j,\mu}\xi^{2j+1}$, $\sigma_\mu = \sum_j s_{j,\mu}\xi^{2j+2}$, $c_\nu = \sum_j g_{j,\nu}\xi^{2j}$ with $d_{0,\mu} = 2F_\mu$,
$c = 1 + O(\xi^2)$ is unique and Gevrey-1 in $z = \xi^2$. We write $\mathsf y = (y_{\pm1}, \sigma_{\pm1}, c_{0,\pm2})$ for the
state.

\begin{definition}[lateral solutions and the selection condition]\label{def:M1sel}
Let $\bx$ be real. The \emph{lateral solution} $\mathsf y^+$ is the solution asymptotic to the formal series on a sector of small
radius containing $0 \le \arg z \le 3\pi/4$; its existence and uniqueness are given by Braaksma's
theorem~\cite{Braaksma1992} (Lemma~\ref{lem:B4}), \emph{the one fact taken from the literature}. The reflection
$\mathcal R(\mathsf y)_\mu = \overline{\mathsf y_{-\mu}}$ (on each of $y$, $\sigma$, $c$) maps solutions to solutions under
$\xi \mapsto \bar\xi$ and fixes the formal series, so $\mathsf y^-(\xi) = \mathcal R(\mathsf y^+(\bar\xi))$ is the lateral solution
from below. The \emph{$M = 1$ selection condition} is $\mathsf y^+ \equiv \mathsf y^-$. With $\mathsf y^\pm_{\mathbb R}$ the
continuations along the real axis, put
\[
\psi = \mathsf y^+_{\mathbb R} - \mathsf y^-_{\mathbb R} = \mathsf y^+_{\mathbb R} - \mathcal R(\mathsf y^+_{\mathbb R}),\qquad
J(\xi_1; \bx) = \psi_{y,+1}(\xi_1) = y^+_{+1}(\xi_1) - \overline{y^+_{-1}(\xi_1)} ,
\]
so that $\psi_{y,-1}(\xi_1) = -\overline{J(\xi_1)}$ and $\mathcal R\psi = -\psi$. ($J$ is $J_D$ in~\repo{proof/A\_certify.md}.) The
single complex Stokes constant $\Sigma(\bx)$ is defined in \eqref{eq:Sigma} below.
\end{definition}

In the linear problem the jump is known in closed form (Section~\ref{sec:inner}), and in ball arithmetic it equals
$2\pi\iu K'(s)\,2F_1$ times the recessive solution to $1 \pm 3\times10^{-30}$. The computation follows a path: it continues $\mathsf y^+$
from a start point $\xi_0 = 0.1\,e^{3\iu\pi/8}$ on the ray $\arg\xi = 3\pi/8$, along that ray and then down to the real axis.
Define $J^{\rm path}(0.3; \bx)$ as $J$ computed with $\mathsf y^+$ continued along the \emph{lower path}: the ray from $0.1$ to $0.2$,
the arc $|\xi| = 0.2$ down to the real axis, and the real axis from $0.2$ to $0.3$.

\subsection{The template and the plan of the proof}
The template is the computer-assisted proof of a Stokes constant by Baldom\'a, Capi\'nski, Guardia and
Seara~\cite{BaldomaCapinskiGuardiaSeara2023}: two solutions characterized by their asymptotics on two sectorial domains;
a fixed point for each in a weighted space, with explicit bounds on the forcing and on a Lipschitz constant; validated
values far out; and the Stokes constant from the difference of the two solutions, by a contraction for that difference.
We need a \emph{zero} of the multiplier in two real parameters, not a non-zero value, so an interval-Newton (Krawczyk) step
is added. The proof has these parts:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item a start-value fixed point encloses $\mathsf y^+$ near $\xi = 0$, with parameter derivatives (Lemma~\ref{lem:start});
\item a validated Lohner integration carries the enclosure along the path to $\xi_1 = 0.3$;
\item $J$ comes from the same enclosure, using the reflection;
\item a Krawczyk test shows that $J^{\rm path}(0.3; \cdot)$ has exactly one zero in the box, and that it is simple;
\end{enumerate}
together with two lemmas: \emph{6b}, that the path value is the value of the real-axis continuation (analyticity of $\mathsf y^+$
on the region the path sweeps; Lemmas~\ref{lem:S}, \ref{lem:6b0}, \ref{lem:6b1}), and \emph{6a}, that the one component $J$
at the one point $0.3$ vanishes only if the whole jump does (Lemma~\ref{lem:6a}). Everything is computed in ball arithmetic
(the Arb library through python-flint 0.9, $128$--$320$ bits); every input decimal (the centre of the box, its half-width,
$0.1$, $0.3$, $1.64389375$) enters as a decimal string, so every ball contains the exact decimal.

\subsection{Step (i): the start-value fixed point}
Let $z = \xi^2$ and, on each harmonic, $y_\mu = \xi P_\mu$, $\sigma_\mu = zQ_\mu$, $V_\mu = Q_\mu - (1+s_\mu)P_\mu$ with
$s_\mu = \iu\mu\omega$; let $W = V + [(C-1)Q]$ and $\hat E = [P^2/2]$, where $C$ denotes $c$. For $z \ne 0$, \eqref{eq:M1} is
equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PVC}
\begin{gathered}
P' = \frac{V + [(C-1)Q]}{2z}, \qquad C' = \tfrac12\,[C\hat E] ,\\
V' = \frac{V}{2z^2} + \frac{[(C-1)Q] + [(C-1)W]}{2z^2} + \frac{-(2+s)\,Q - (1+s)\,W + [C\hat EP]}{2z},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $' = \dd/\dd z$. The only rank-one singular term is $V/(2z^2)$, whose solution $e^{-1/(2z)}$ is the fast mode; the slow
fundamental solutions are constants, and every other term is a perturbation, since $C - 1 = O(z)$.

Write $\mathsf Y = \mathsf Y_N + \eta$, with $\mathsf Y_N$ the formal series truncated at $N = 53$ terms and
$\eta = (\eta_P, \eta_V, \eta_C)$, and let $\Phi_\bullet$ be the right-hand side minus the principal term $V/(2z^2)$, evaluated
at $\mathsf Y_N + \eta$, minus $\mathsf Y_N'$; thus $\Phi = \rho_N + (\text{the change of the right-hand side})$, with $\rho_N$ the
residual of the truncated series. Integrating from $z = 0$ along a ray,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inteq}
\eta_P(z) = \int_0^z \Phi_P\,\dd t, \qquad
\eta_V(z) = \int_0^z e^{\frac{1}{2t} - \frac{1}{2z}}\,\Phi_V(t)\,\dd t, \qquad
\eta_C(z) = \int_0^z \Phi_C\,\dd t .
\end{equation}
On the ray $\arg z = \vartheta$ the fast kernel has modulus $e^{-a(1/|t| - 1/|z|)} \le 1$ for $|t| \le |z|$, with
$a = -\tfrac12\cos\vartheta = \sqrt2/4$ at $\vartheta = \pm3\pi/4$, and with $r = |z|$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernels}
\int_0^r \varrho^m\,\dd\varrho = \frac{r^{m+1}}{m+1}\ \ (m > -1), \qquad
\int_0^r e^{-a\left(\frac1\varrho - \frac1r\right)}\varrho^m\,\dd\varrho \le \frac{r^{m+2}}{a}\ \ (m \ge -2) ,
\end{equation}
the second by the substitution $1/\varrho$ and $\varrho^m \le r^{m+2}\varrho^{-2}$. On a ray at angle $\vartheta$ the rate $a$
stays positive for $\pi/2 < \vartheta < 3\pi/2$; on $|\arg\xi \mp 3\pi/8| \le 0.2$ its smallest value is $0.188$.

Let $R = 0.01$ and $k = 40$, and use on $0 < r \le R$ the norms $\sup r^{-k}|\eta_P|$, $\sup r^{-k}|\eta_V|$ and
$\sup r^{-k-1}|\eta_C|$ for each of the seven components, combined into a weighted maximum with weights from the Perron vector
of the Lipschitz matrix (any positive weights are valid). Every function is bounded by a majorant $K|t|^q$; products and
harmonic convolutions are bounded by the triangle inequality, derivatives by the product rule on majorants, and the
Lipschitz matrix by the mean-value inequality for holomorphic maps on the convex ball. Every bound is an upper bound in
ball arithmetic and non-decreasing in $R$.

\begin{lemma}[start values]\label{lem:start}
Let $\bx$ be the point $\hx = (2.5421480475, 0.7769940136)$ or any point of the complex box around it of half-width
$3\times10^{-2}$ in the real and in the imaginary part of each parameter. On each ray $\arg\xi = \pm3\pi/8$ with
$0 < |\xi| \le 0.1$, the truncated system has a solution $\mathsf Y$ with $\mathsf Y - \mathsf Y_N$ in an explicit ball of the weighted
space, and it is the only solution in that ball; every solution with $\mathsf Y - \mathsf Y_N = o(|\xi|^{82})$ along the ray is this one,
and $\mathsf y^+$ (on the upper ray) and $\mathsf y^-$ (on the lower ray) are such solutions. At $\xi_0 = 0.1\,e^{3\iu\pi/8}$ and $\hx$,
the enclosure has radius $2.4\times10^{-34}$ in $y$, $6.0\times10^{-34}$ in $\sigma$ and $5.9\times10^{-36}$ in $c$ on every harmonic. The
same holds on every ray with $|\arg\xi \mp 3\pi/8| \le 0.2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Forcing.} The numerators of $\rho_N$ are polynomials in $z$ whose coefficients below order $53$ (for $P$ and $V$) and
$52$ (for $C$) vanish identically, because the recursion solves the order-by-order equations exactly; the computation
checks that each of these balls contains $0$, and the independent review verified the vanishing exactly in
$\mathrm{GF}(p)$ at random parameters (the chance of a false pass is below $10^{-50}$). The rest is
bounded and pushed through \eqref{eq:kernels}, which gives $F = \|T(0)\|$ for the operator $T$ of \eqref{eq:inteq}. For
$|\xi| \le 0.1$ the residual itself is at most $1.0\times10^{-33}$ in every direction.

\emph{Contraction.} With $\Lambda$ the Lipschitz constant of $T$ on the ball of radius $\varrho$, the check is $\Lambda < 1$ and
$F + \Lambda\varrho < \varrho$:
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
case & $\Lambda \le$ & $F$ & $\varrho$ & $F + \Lambda\varrho$ \\
\midrule
$\hx$, rays $\arg\xi = \pm3\pi/8$ & $0.2918$ & $1.32\times10^{48}$ & $7.43\times10^{48}$ & $3.48\times10^{48}$ \\
complex box $\pm10^{-3}(1+\iu)$ & $0.2923$ & $8.57\times10^{69}$ & $4.84\times10^{70}$ & $2.27\times10^{70}$ \\
complex box $\pm3\times10^{-2}(1+\iu)$ & $0.3048$ & $1.17\times10^{72}$ & $6.73\times10^{72}$ & $3.22\times10^{72}$ \\
$\hx$, all rays $|\arg\xi \mp 3\pi/8| \le 0.2$ & $0.5107$ & $1.32\times10^{48}$ & $1.08\times10^{49}$ & $6.80\times10^{48}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
($F$ and $\varrho$ are in the weighted norm and carry the factor $R^{-k} = 10^{80}$; in absolute terms the ball at $\hx$ is
$|\eta| \le 5.3\times10^{-32}$.) So $T$ maps the ball into itself and contracts.

\emph{Identification.} Every bound is non-decreasing in $R$, so the contraction holds on each $(0, R']$, $R' \le R$, and,
the operator being of Volterra type, the fixed point on $(0, R]$ restricts to the fixed point on $(0, R']$. A solution with
$\eta_P, \eta_V = o(r^k)$ and $\eta_C = o(r^{k+1})$ satisfies \eqref{eq:inteq}; the boundary terms vanish because
$e^{1/(2\epsilon)} \to 0$ as $\epsilon \to 0$ along the ray, where the fast mode is exponentially large. Its weighted norm on
$(0, R']$ tends to $0$, so for small $R'$ it lies in the ball and is the fixed point there, and by uniqueness for the regular
ODE it is the fixed point on all of $(0, R]$. Finally $\mathsf y^+ - \mathsf Y_N = O(z^{53})$ on the upper ray by
Definition~\ref{def:M1sel}, and $53 > k + 1$; the lower ray gives $\mathsf y^-$ by the reflection.
\end{proof}

\emph{Parameter derivatives.} The Krawczyk test needs $\partial\mathsf y/\partial\bx$ at $\xi_0$ over a real box $X$. The derivatives of
$\mathsf Y_N$ are exact (forward-mode jets~\cite{Wengert1964} through the recursion). Since the contraction holds uniformly on the complex box
$X + c_0(1+\iu)$, $c_0 = 10^{-3}$, $\eta$ is holomorphic in the parameters there, and Cauchy's estimate gives
$|\partial_{x_i}\eta| \le c_0^{-1}\sup_{X + c_0(1+\iu)}|\eta|$; at $\xi_0$ this adds less than $10^{-8}$ to the derivative enclosures.

\subsection{Step (ii): validated integration}
The integrator (\repo{validated\_int.py}) is a QR-Lohner method~\cite{Lohner1987,NedialkovJackson2001} in ball arithmetic at
$256$ bits, with $\omega$ and $A$ carried as constant states and the set represented in real coordinates as $c + Qq$ ($c$ a point,
$Q$ a real $18\times18$ matrix, $q$ a box); every step is a multiple of $2^{-30}$ of the leg parameter, so every node is exact. One
step consists of an a-priori enclosure of all trajectories from the set by interval Picard iteration; Taylor polynomials of
order $24$ of the centre trajectory and of the variational matrix; remainders from the order-$25$ coefficients on the a-priori
enclosure; and the mean-value update $\phi(c + Qq) \subset \phi(c) + JQq$, re-orthogonalized by the $Q$ factor of
$\operatorname{mid}(JQ)$, whose inverse is enclosed rigorously. The stiff fast modes therefore only shrink $q$, whereas a naive
interval Taylor method would overestimate by about $e^{50}$ on the ray. The columns are pivoted by their norm times the size of the
matching component of $q$ (Lohner's rule); with norms alone the enclosure grew by about $15\%$ per step at the start of the arc,
where the flow is strongly non-normal, against $2$--$3\%$. For the derivatives over a box (the \emph{box run}) each column
$\partial\mathsf y/\partial x_k$ is a Lohner set of its own, started from the data of step (i), and the state set over the box is rebuilt at
every step as $\mathsf y(t;\hx) + V(t;X)(\bx - \hx)$, with $V$ the derivative enclosure.

On the path ray--arc at radius $0.3$ (743 steps), the point run gives $J(\hx) = 1.054740\times10^{-12} - 3.490429\times10^{-12}\iu$ with
radius $4.1\times10^{-19}$, in agreement with a double-precision solver to $9\times10^{-16}$; on the lower path (303, 322 and 47
steps on the three legs) the same $J(\hx)$ to the digits shown, with radius $6\times10^{-17}$. The box runs are the
bottleneck: the excess of the derivative enclosures grows like $7\times10^5\,h$ in the half-width $h$ of the box, because the
solution is large where the ray meets the arc, and they diverge for $h \ge 10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Step (iv): the Krawczyk test}
Let $X = \hx + [-h, h]^2$ and $g(\bx) = \kappa_0\,J^{\rm path}(0.3; \bx)$, read as a map $\mathbb R^2 \to \mathbb R^2$, where
$\kappa_0 = \xi_1^{-\hat A^2}/N(\hx)$ is a non-zero constant enclosed rigorously, $N$ being the linear normalization
$2\pi\iu K'(s)\,2F_1$ times the recessive solution at $\xi_1$. Let $J(X)$ enclose $\{Dg(\bx) : \bx \in X\}$ (from the box run;
for real $\bx$ the derivative of the conjugate is the conjugate of the derivative), and let $Y$ be any real $2\times2$ matrix (the
code takes a floating-point inverse of $\operatorname{mid}J(X)$). Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:krawczyk}
K(X) = \hx - Y\,g(\hx) + \big(I - Y\,J(X)\big)(X - \hx) .
\end{equation}
If $K(X) \subset \operatorname{int}X$, then $g$ has exactly one zero in $X$ and $Dg$ is non-singular there
(Krawczyk~\cite{Krawczyk1969}, Moore~\cite{Moore1977}, Neumaier~\cite{Neumaier1990}). The inputs are $g$ at the single point
$\hx$ and $J$ over the whole box.

\emph{Results.} With $h = 10^{-9}$ the test passes on the ray--arc path at radius $0.3$, with margins $7.9\times10^{-10}$ and
$9.2\times10^{-10}$ (out of $10^{-9}$); there $J(X)$ has midpoints $-15.32$, $-103.12$, $90.86$, $-44.09$ and radii $2.7$--$3.5$, about
a tenth of what the test tolerates. On the lower path it passes with margins $8.570\times10^{-10}$ and $9.712\times10^{-10}$; since
$J(X)$ also encloses the Jacobian over every sub-box centred at $\hx$, it passes on $\hx \pm 1.2\times10^{-10}$ as well, which
puts the zero of $J^{\rm path}(0.3;\cdot)$ in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zerobox}
A \in [2.54214804761338,\ 2.54214804761975], \qquad \omega/\winf \in [0.776994013607438,\ 0.776994013612010]
\end{equation}
(endpoints rounded outward; $\omega \approx 2.96977670855$). Larger boxes fail because of the derivative excess; a $C^2$ Lohner
step would be needed for $10^{-3}$ boxes.

\subsection{Lemma S and the analyticity near the origin}
The rays of Lemma~\ref{lem:start} stay at $|\arg z - 3\pi/4| \le 0.4$. To reach the real axis, change the integration path of
$\eta_V$ and nothing else. Let $R_+ = 0.01$, $U_0 = \sqrt2/R_+$, $\mathcal K = \{w : -3\pi/4 \le \arg w \le 0\}$, and
\[
\mathcal D_S = \{z : 1/z \in U_0 + \mathcal K\}.
\]
Elementary geometry of the translated cone gives: $|z| \le R_+$ on $\mathcal D_S$; in the direction $\arg z = \vartheta \in [0, 3\pi/4)$,
$\mathcal D_S$ contains every $z$ with $|z| \le R_+\sin(3\pi/4 - \vartheta)$, which is at least $R_+/\sqrt2$ for $\vartheta \le \pi/2$ and
includes the real segment $0 < z \le R_+/\sqrt2$; $\mathcal D_S$ is star-shaped about $0$, so $\eta_P$ and $\eta_C$ are still
integrated along $[0, z]$; and for $u \in U_0 + \mathcal K$ the path $u + s'e^{-3\iu\pi/4}$, $s' \ge 0$, stays in $U_0 + \mathcal K$ and
ends at $z \to 0$ along $\arg z \to 3\pi/4$. For $z \in \mathcal D_S$ integrate the $V$ equation along that path:
$\eta_V(z) = \int_{\gamma_z}e^{1/(2t) - 1/(2z)}\Phi_V(t)\,\dd t$ with $1/t = 1/z + s'e^{-3\iu\pi/4}$, $s'$ from $\infty$ to $0$. Along it
the kernel has modulus $e^{-as'}$, $a = \sqrt2/4$, and $|\dd t| = |t|^2\dd s'$. With $u = 1/z$, $c_\phi = -\cos\phi$ ($\phi$ the angle
between $u$ and $e^{-3\iu\pi/4}$) and $X' = s'/|u|$,
\[
\frac{|u|^2}{|u + s'e^{-3\iu\pi/4}|^2} = \frac{1}{1 - 2c_\phi X' + X'^2} \le e^{2c_\phi X'}\qquad (0 \le c_\phi \le 1/\sqrt2),
\]
because $f(X') = \ln(1 - 2c_\phi X' + X'^2) + 2c_\phi X'$ has $f(0) = 0$ and $f' = 2X'(1 - 2c_\phi^2 + c_\phi X')/(1 - 2c_\phi X' + X'^2) \ge 0$;
for $c_\phi \le 0$ the ratio is at most $1$. On $\mathcal D_S$, $c_\phi \le 1/\sqrt2$ always and $c_\phi/|u| \le 1/(\sqrt2U_0) = R_+/2$. Hence
\[
\int_{\gamma_z}\Big|e^{\frac1{2t} - \frac1{2z}}\Big|\,|t|^m\,|\dd t| \le |z|^{m+2}\int_0^\infty e^{-as' + (m+2)\frac{c_\phi}{|u|}s'}\,\dd s'
\le \frac{|z|^{m+2}}{a - (m+2)R_+/2},
\]
and in the weighted norm the fast bound takes the form of \eqref{eq:kernels} with $a$ replaced by
$a_{\rm eff} = \sqrt2/4 - kR_+/2$. The Perron weights are made symmetric under $\mu \mapsto -\mu$, so that $\mathcal R$ maps the ball to
itself.

\begin{lemnamed}{S}\label{lem:S}
Let $X = \hx + [-10^{-9}, 10^{-9}]^2$. For every $\bx \in X$ the truncated system has a solution $\mathsf Y = \mathsf Y_N + \eta$ on
$\mathcal D_S$, with $\eta$ analytic in the interior of $\mathcal D_S$ and in an explicit weighted ball; it is unique in that ball, and it
is $\mathsf y^+$. (With $k = 30$, $N = 53$: $a_{\rm eff} = 0.20355$, contraction constant at most $0.4903$, forcing $1.04\times10^{44}$,
ball $8.14\times10^{44}$, and $F + \Lambda\varrho = 5.03\times10^{44}$.)
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
The contraction is checked as in Lemma~\ref{lem:start} (\repo{lemma6\_sector.py}). For the identification, the domains
$\mathcal D_S(U_0') = \{1/z \in U_0' + \mathcal K\}$ decrease as $U_0'$ grows, the operator on $\mathcal D_S(U_0')$ uses only values there,
and every bound is non-decreasing in $R_+$, so the contraction holds on each $\mathcal D_S(U_0')$, $U_0' \ge U_0$. For large $U_0'$,
$\mathcal D_S(U_0')$ lies in the sector of asymptoticity of $\mathsf y^+$, where $\mathsf y^+ - \mathsf Y_N = O(|z|^{53})$; so $\mathsf y^+ - \mathsf Y_N$
satisfies the integral equation there (the boundary terms of $\eta_P$, $\eta_C$ vanish at $0$, and that of $\eta_V$ is $e^{-as'}$
times a bounded function), its weighted norm tends to $0$, and it is the fixed point. Both are solutions of the ODE on the
connected set $\mathcal D_S$, so they agree on $\mathcal D_S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemnamed}{6b$_0$}\label{lem:6b0}
For every $\bx \in X$, $\mathsf y^+$ extends analytically to the interior of $\Omega_0 = \{0 < |\xi| \le 0.0624,\ 0 \le \arg\xi \le 3\pi/8\}$,
continuously to its boundary, and to the real segment $0 < \xi \le 0.0841$, where it is enclosed by Lemma~S.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
$\mathcal D_S$ contains the directions $0 \le \arg z \le 3\pi/4 - 0.4$ up to radius at least $R_+\sin0.4 = 0.003894$, i.e.\
$|\xi| \le 0.0624$, and the real segment up to $R_+/\sqrt2$, i.e.\ $\xi \le 0.0841$. The sector version of
Lemma~\ref{lem:start}, re-proved over $X$ (contraction $0.5107$), covers $3\pi/4 - 0.4 \le \arg z \le 3\pi/4$ up to $|z| = 0.01$; its
fixed points on these rays form one analytic function, because the ray integrals from $0$ are path-independent there
($\re(1/t) \to -\infty$ uniformly as $t \to 0$ in that sector), and it is $\mathsf y^+$. The two pieces overlap in a connected set
on which both are $\mathsf y^+$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lemma 6a: one component at one point is the whole jump}
Put $\xi_a = 0.067$ and $Z = \xi_a^2 = 0.004489 \le R_+/\sqrt2$. In the variables of \eqref{eq:PVC}, let $\beta = \eta_V$-component of
$\psi$ at $z = Z$, $\beta \in \mathbb C^2$; it satisfies $\beta_{-1} = -\overline{\beta_{+1}}$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma}
\Sigma(\bx) := \beta_{+1} = (\text{fast component of the jump } \psi \text{ on harmonic } +1 \text{ at } z = Z),
\end{equation}
the single complex Stokes constant of the $M = 1$ problem.

\begin{lemnamed}{6a}\label{lem:6a}
Let $B_1$ be the state-set box at $\xi = 0.3$ of the lower-path box run. For every $\bx \in X$: if $\mathsf y^+_{\mathbb R}(0.3;\bx) \in B_1$,
then
\[
J(0.3;\bx) = 0 \iff \psi \equiv 0 \iff \mathsf y^+ \equiv \mathsf y^- \iff \Sigma(\bx) = 0 .
\]
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
The implications from right to left are immediate except $\Sigma = 0 \Rightarrow \psi \equiv 0$, which is step~1.

\emph{Step 1 (flat estimate on $0 < z \le Z$).} By Lemma~S, $\mathsf y^+ = \mathsf Y_N + \eta^+$ there with $\eta^+$ in the ball; by the
reflection and the symmetric weights $\mathsf y^- = \mathsf Y_N + \mathcal R(\eta^+)$ is in the same ball, and so is the segment between them.
So $\psi = \eta^+ - \eta^-$ satisfies exactly $\psi = b + T_0\psi$, with $b = (0, e^{1/(2Z) - 1/(2z)}\beta, 0)$, where $T_0$ integrates the
difference of the perturbation terms (linear in $\psi$ by the mean-value theorem on the ball): the slow components from $0$
(valid because $\psi = O(z^k)$), and the fast one backward from $Z$ by variation of constants. The backward kernel obeys, for
$m + 2 \ge 0$ and $(m+2)Z < \tfrac12$,
\[
\int_z^Z e^{-\frac12\left(\frac1z - \frac1t\right)}\,t^m\,\dd t \le \frac{z^{m+2}}{\tfrac12 - (m+2)Z}
\]
(substitute $1/t$ and use $(U/u)^{m+2} \le e^{(m+2)Z(U - u)}$), so the weighted fast bound has $a_{\rm eff} = \tfrac12 - kZ = 0.3653$,
and with the derivative majorants over the ball of Lemma~S, $\|T_0\| \le \Lambda = 0.1513$. Since $z^{-k}e^{-1/(2z)}$ increases on
$(0, Z]$, $\|b\| = Z^{-k}\max_\mu\mathsf w_{V,\mu}|\beta_\mu|$, with $\mathsf w$ the weights. Hence $\epsilon := \|\psi - b\| \le
\Lambda(1-\Lambda)^{-1}\|b\|$, and $|\psi_{P,\mu}(Z)| \le Z^k\epsilon/\mathsf w_{P,\mu}$, $|\psi_{C,\nu}(Z)| \le Z^{k+1}\epsilon/\mathsf w_{C,\nu}$, while the
fast component at $Z$ is $\beta$. In particular $\beta = 0$ gives $\psi = T_0\psi$, hence $\psi = 0$ on $(0, Z]$.

\emph{Step 2 (the backward flow).} If $J(0.3) = 0$, then $\psi(0.3) = (0, 0, \mathbf u)$, the $y$-components vanishing, with
$\mathbf u = (\psi_\sigma, \psi_c) \in \mathbb C^5$. The $C^1$ Lohner integrator runs backward along the real axis from $0.3$ to $\xi_a$, where
the fast modes decay by about $e^{-106}$, from the hull of $B_1$ and $\mathcal R(B_1)$ with the identity as variational columns
(291 steps; largest radius $1.8\times10^{-6}$). The resulting enclosure $B$ contains the flow Jacobian at every point of that hull,
so by the mean-value theorem $\psi(\xi_a) \in B\,\psi(0.3)$. In the variables of \eqref{eq:PVC} at $\xi_a$, with the exact change of
variables $T_a$ and $G = T_aB$ restricted to the columns of $\mathbf u$, $(\psi_P, \psi_C)(Z) = G_{\rm slow}\mathbf u$ and $\beta = G_{\rm fast}\mathbf u$.

\emph{Step 3 (contraction in $\beta$).} $G_{\rm slow}$ ($5\times5$) is invertible: the ball is inverted rigorously, with entries of
$G_{\rm slow}^{-1}$ at most $1.26$. So $\beta = H\,(\psi_P, \psi_C)(Z)$ with $H = G_{\rm fast}G_{\rm slow}^{-1}$, whose entries are at most
$7.7$. With step 1,
\[
\max_\mu\mathsf w_{V,\mu}|\beta_\mu| \le \rho_{6a}\,\max_\mu\mathsf w_{V,\mu}|\beta_\mu|, \qquad
\rho_{6a} = \frac{\Lambda}{1-\Lambda}\,\max_\mu\mathsf w_{V,\mu}\sum_j|H_{\mu j}|\,\frac{Z^{k_j-k}}{\mathsf w_j} \le 4.22\times10^{-3} .
\]
So $\beta = 0$, and $\psi = 0$ on $(0, Z]$ by step 1. By uniqueness for the ODE, $\mathsf y^+_{\mathbb R} \equiv \mathsf y^-_{\mathbb R}$, and the two
germs coincide.
\end{proof}

The margin is large ($\rho_{6a} = 0.004$ against $1$). A relative change of $10^{-6}$ in one series coefficient makes Lemma~S fail, and
dropping the relabelling $\mu \mapsto -\mu$ in the reflection makes the backward integration fail; but $(1 - \iu\mu\omega)$ in place of
$(1 + \iu\mu\omega)$ in the change of variables at $\xi_a$ is not caught ($\rho_{6a} = 0.12$), since the wrong $V$ differs by
$2\iu\mu\omega P$ and $P(Z)$ is itself small. That formula is guarded instead by self-tests against the integrator's system.

\subsection{Lemma 6b\texorpdfstring{$_1$}{1}: a covering by validated Taylor discs}
It remains to show that $\mathsf y^+$ is analytic on the annular sector between $|\xi| = 0.0624$ and $0.2$. A first-order family
of paths (tubes swept with the offset as a state) blows up beyond widths of about $0.1$--$0.2\%$ of the radius, because the
parameter width enters every step Jacobian. Instead we use that a translated family $\Psi(t, d) = \mathsf y(u_c(t) + d)$ is the Taylor
expansion of $\mathsf y$ around $u_c(t)$, whose natural radius is the distance to the nearest singularity of $\mathsf y^+$. Work in
$u = 1/\xi^2$, where the fast mode is $e^{-u/2}$ with a constant rate, with the state
$\mathsf Y = (P_{-1}, P_{+1}, V_{-1}, V_{+1}, 100C_{-2}, 100(C_0 - 1), 100C_{+2})$; the system $\mathsf Y' = \mathcal F(u, \mathsf Y; \mathbf p)$ is polynomial
in $\mathsf Y$ and in $1/u$, with parameters $\mathbf p$. In double precision the nearest singularity of $\mathsf y^+$ is a branch point at
$u^* = 9.6746\,e^{-2.1198\iu}$ ($\xi^* = 0.3215\,e^{1.0599\iu}$), at distance $15$ or more from the annulus.

\begin{lemnamed}{D (disc lemma)}\label{lem:D}
Let $c$ and $\mathbf p$ be complex balls, $\mathsf Y_0$ a box in $\mathbb C^7$, $N_T \ge 1$ and $0 < r < |c|$, and suppose the computation
below succeeds. Then for every exact $c' \in c$, $\mathsf y_0 \in \mathsf Y_0$ and $\mathbf p' \in \mathbf p$, the solution with $\mathsf y(c') = \mathsf y_0$
extends analytically to the open disc $|u - c'| < r$, continuously to its closure, and $|\mathsf y_j(u) - \mathsf y_{0,j}| \le M_j$ there.
\end{lemnamed}

The computation: (1) the Taylor coefficients $p_k$, $k \le N_T$, of the solution in $\tau = u - c$, by Picard iteration on power
series in ball arithmetic from the balls; they enclose the Taylor coefficients of every solution in the statement.
(2) The defect $d = \mathcal F(c + \tau, p(\tau)) - p'(\tau)$ as a polynomial, with $1/u$ expanded as
$\sum_{j\le J}(-\tau)^j/c^{j+1} + \tau^{J+1}\zeta(\tau)$, $|\zeta(\tau)| \le |c|^{-J-2}(1 - r/|c|)^{-1}$, $J = N_T + 8$, and $\zeta$ replaced by
a ball of that radius. (3) The coefficients of $d$ below order $N_T$ vanish identically for each exact initial value; the code
checks that their balls contain $0$ and drops them, so $|d_i(\tau)| \le \Delta_i|\tau|^{N_T}$ with
$\Delta_i = \sum_{k\ge N_T}|d_{i,k}|r^{k-N_T}$. (4) With $M^0_j = \sum_{1\le k\le N_T}|p_{j,k}|r^k$, the box
$B_j = \mathsf Y_{0,j} + \operatorname{disc}(M^0_j + b_j)$, a ball containing $1/u$ on the disc, and $L_{ij} \ge |\partial\mathcal F_i/\partial\mathsf y_j|$
over all of these, form, with positive weights $\mathsf w$,
\[
\Lambda = \frac{r}{N_T+2}\,\max_i\sum_j\frac{\mathsf w_iL_{ij}}{\mathsf w_j}, \qquad F_0 = \max_i\frac{\mathsf w_i\Delta_i}{N_T+1}, \qquad
\varrho = \frac{2F_0}{1-\Lambda},
\]
and check $\Lambda < 1$, $F_0 + \Lambda\varrho \le \varrho$ and $\varrho r^{N_T+1}/\mathsf w_j \le b_j$; then $M_j = M^0_j + b_j$.

\begin{proof}
The remainder $R = \mathsf y - p$ solves $R = T(R)$, $T(R)(\tau) = \int_0^\tau[d(\tau') + \mathcal F(c' + \tau', p + R) - \mathcal F(c' + \tau', p)]\,\dd\tau'$
along the segment $[0, \tau]$. On the Banach space of $R$ analytic in the open disc and continuous on its closure, with norm
$\max_i\mathsf w_i\sup_{|\tau|\le r}|R_i(\tau)|/|\tau|^{N_T+1}$, every $R$ with $\|R\| \le \varrho$ has $|R_j| \le \varrho r^{N_T+1}/\mathsf w_j \le b_j$, so
$p + R$ stays in the convex box $B$, where the mean-value inequality gives
$|\mathcal F_i(p + R) - \mathcal F_i(p + R')| \le \sum_jL_{ij}|R_j - R'_j|$. Integrating $|\tau'|^{N_T}$ and $|\tau'|^{N_T+1}$ from $0$ to $|\tau|$ gives
$\|T(0)\| \le F_0$ and $\|T(R) - T(R')\| \le \Lambda\|R - R'\|$, so $T$ maps the ball into itself and contracts; its fixed point gives
$\mathsf y = p + R$, analytic in the disc, with $\mathsf y(c') = \mathsf y_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemnamed}{G (gluing)}\label{lem:G}
Let $\mathsf y^+$ be analytic on an open set $G_0$. Let $T_1, \dots, T_K$ be open sets, and let each $f_m$ solve the system and be analytic
on an open disc $D_m \supset T_m$. Suppose that for each $m$ the set $M_m = T_m \cap (G_0 \cup T_1 \cup\dots\cup T_{m-1})$ is connected
and contains a point $q_m$ with $f_m(q_m) = f(q_m)$, where $f$ is $\mathsf y^+$ if $q_m \in G_0$ and otherwise the $f_l$ of an earlier tile
$T_l \ni q_m$. Then $\mathsf y^+$ extends analytically to $G_0 \cup T_1 \cup\dots\cup T_K$ and equals $f_m$ on $T_m$.
\end{lemnamed}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $m$: $f_m$ and the function built so far both solve the system on the connected open set $M_m$ and agree at
$q_m$, so they agree near $q_m$ (the system is regular for $u \ne 0$) and hence on $M_m$ by the identity theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The theorem}

\begin{thmnamed}{A($M = 1$)}\label{thm:A}
Assume Braaksma's $1$-summability theorem for the existence of the lateral solutions $\mathsf y^\pm$ and their asymptotics
(Definition~\ref{def:M1sel}; the one fact taken from the literature). Let $\bx = (A, \omega/\winf)$, $\winf = 2\pi/1.64389375$, and
$X = (2.5421480475, 0.7769940136) + [-10^{-9}, 10^{-9}]^2$. Then the $M = 1$ selection condition has exactly one zero in $X$, it is
simple, and it lies at
\[
A \in [2.54214804761338,\ 2.54214804761975], \qquad \omega/\winf \in [0.776994013607438,\ 0.776994013612010]
\]
($\omega \approx 2.96977670855$). Precisely:
\begin{itemize}
\item there is exactly one $\bx^* \in X$ with $\mathsf y^+ \equiv \mathsf y^-$, equivalently with $\Sigma(\bx^*) = 0$, equivalently with
$J(0.3; \bx^*) = 0$;
\item $\bx^*$ lies in the box above;
\item the real $2\times2$ Jacobians of $\Sigma$ and of $\bx \mapsto J(0.3; \bx)$ are non-singular at $\bx^*$.
\end{itemize}
\end{thmnamed}

\begin{proof}
(1) \emph{$J^{\rm path} = J$.} Lemma~6b$_0$ gives analyticity of $\mathsf y^+$ on the part $|\xi| \le 0.0624$ of
$\Omega = \{0 < |\xi| \le 0.2,\ 0 \le \arg\xi \le 3\pi/8\}$, Lemma~6b$_1$ on a neighbourhood of the annular sector, and the two overlap in
$G_0$, where both are $\mathsf y^+$. So $\mathsf y^+$ is analytic on $\Omega$, the region between the lower path and the real axis; the
continuation along the lower path equals the continuation along the real axis, and $J^{\rm path}(0.3;\bx) = J(0.3;\bx)$ for every
$\bx \in X$. (2) \emph{One simple zero.} By the Krawczyk test on the lower path, $J^{\rm path}(0.3;\cdot)$ has exactly one zero in
$X$; it is simple and lies in the box \eqref{eq:zerobox}. (3) \emph{It is the selection condition.} By (1), $\mathsf y^+_{\mathbb R}(0.3;\bx)$
is the path value, so it lies in the state set $B_1$ of the box run; that is the hypothesis of Lemma~6a, which gives
$J(0.3;\bx) = 0 \Leftrightarrow \mathsf y^+ \equiv \mathsf y^- \Leftrightarrow \Sigma(\bx) = 0$. (4) \emph{Simplicity.} At $\bx^*$,
$\psi \equiv 0$, so $\varphi_j = \partial\psi/\partial x_j$ solves the variational equation along $\mathsf y^+ = \mathsf y^-$, with finite weighted norm
(each $\psi(\cdot;\bx)$ obeys the flat estimate). The argument of Lemma~6a applies to $\varphi_j$ verbatim, since its coefficients
lie in the same hulls. So the real-linear map $\mathcal L : \partial\Sigma \mapsto \partial J(0.3)$ is injective,
$DJ(0.3;\bx^*) = \mathcal L\circ D\Sigma(\bx^*)$, and $D\Sigma(\bx^*)$ is non-singular.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What is not claimed}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is a statement about the one-harmonic truncation ($M = 1$), not about the physical problem: its zero is
$22\%$ below $\winf$ in frequency, and it does not survive the addition of harmonic $3$. Nothing is said about parameters outside
$X$. The disc covering has no second implementation. At $M = 5$ the higher harmonics leak into the lower conditions with weights up
to $10^{17}$, so a certificate there must be posed on the Schur-corrected conditions, with multiprecision Lohner integration.

\section{The untruncated multiplier as an entire function}
\label{sec:entire}

\subsection{The amplitude expansion}
\label{sec:amp}
Truncating harmonics is not a controlled approximation (Section~\ref{sec:inner}), but there is an expansion in which every
harmonic is kept exactly. Write
\[
F = A\sin\theta + \sum_{\nu\ge3} f_\nu(A)\,e^{\iu\nu\theta} + \text{c.c.}
\]
Harmonic $\mu$ first appears at order $A^{|\mu|}$, so keeping $|\mu| \le K$ at each order in $A$ is exact. At each order the
inner equations are linear in the new unknowns, with forcing from lower orders; the late orders in $j$ of each harmonic are
normalized by their exact linear form $\Gamma(\alpha + j - \delta/2)\Gamma(\beta + j - \delta/2)$, $\delta = \langle F^2\rangle$, de-mixed
by $e^{-\Pi}$, and fitted with every neighbour family $\nu'$, $|\nu'| \le k$, present (they enter through the label $\theta_0$ at
relative order $1/j$). Tuning $f_\nu$ order by order so that $S_\nu = 0$ for all $|\nu| \ge 3$, the harmonic-1 multiplier becomes
a power series in $A^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmak}
S_1/S_1^{(1)} = 1 + \sigma_1A^2 + \sigma_2A^4 + \dots ,
\end{equation}
with $S_1^{(1)}$ the linear multiplier. The coefficients are computed in ball arithmetic at $640$ bits from late-order fits;
at $\omega = \winf$ two fit windows, $J = 750$ and $1000$ terms, agree to about $10^{-19}$:
\begin{gather*}
\sigma_1 = 0.74358457992 + 0.52447485276\iu,\quad \sigma_2 = 0.11879383784 + 0.39807457035\iu,\\
\sigma_3 = -0.05397185243 + 0.11658742432\iu,\quad \sigma_4 = -0.02886475753 + 0.01077835879\iu,\\
\sigma_5 = -0.00555413676 - 0.00280595708\iu,\\
\sigma_6 = (-2.7853745790 - 10.776628405\iu)\times10^{-4},\quad \sigma_7 = (1.0223846436 - 1.4905053530\iu)\times10^{-4};
\end{gather*}
$\sigma_1$--$\sigma_{12}$ are available at $\omega/\winf = 0.99$, $1.00$, $1.01$ (\repo{solver/inner\_amp/}). A fit is accurate but not an enclosure; Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma1} below encloses $\sigma_1$
rigorously. Summing \eqref{eq:sigmak} directly at the zero is hopeless: the terms $|\sigma_k|A_0^{2k}$ grow to about $240$ at
$k = 9$ and are still $160$ at $k = 12$, and would fall below $10^{-6}$ only near $k \approx 37$.

$1/\kappa_\star \approx 0.462 - 0.384\iu$ for $k \ge 8$ (\repo{solver/proof/entire\_cert.py}):

1.00 & $1.279 + 1.081\iu$ & 1.675 & $4.16 - 1.60\iu$ \\

So $|\sigma_k| \approx C|\kappa_\star|^k/\Gamma(k + \beta_\star)$: numerically, $S_1$ is an entire function of $\mathcal A = A^2$ of order $1$ and
exponential type about $1.67$. At $\winf$, $|\sigma_k|\,\Gamma(k + 4.16)/1.675^k = 27.3$, $26.8$, $26.3$, $25.9$, $25.6$, $25.5$, $25.3$,
$25.1$, $24.9$ for $k = 4$--$12$, which suggests the tail bound $|\sigma_k| \le C\,a_\star^k/\Gamma(k + b_\star)$ with $a_\star = 1.675$,
$b_\star = 4.16$, $C = 27.3$; it is calibrated on $k \le 12$, not proved. The Borel transform $B(t) = \sum_k k!\,\sigma_k\,t^{-k-1}$ has, by
Pad\'e approximation, a continuous singular support rather than isolated exponents: an arc from about $0.75 + 0.16\iu$ to
$\kappa_\star$, with a density vanishing at $\kappa_\star$ like $(\kappa_\star - \zeta)^{\beta_\star - 1}$. So $S_1$ behaves like a Laplace integral of
a density over that arc.

Factoring out the arc's centre removes the cancellation. With $G = e^{-\bar\kappa\mathcal A}S_1$ (same zeros), $\bar\kappa$ chosen per

\subsection{What a certificate would need}
A Krawczyk test for $(\re G, \im G)$ in $(A, \omega/\winf)$, where the Jacobian has singular values $0.53$ and $0.26$,
needs the total error below about $2.6\times10^{-3}$ for a box of radius $0.01$ and $2.6\times10^{-4}$ for $10^{-3}$ (Rouch\'e's theorem on
$|\mathcal A - \mathcal A_0| = 0.01$ at fixed $\omega$ needs $5.7\times10^{-4}$). A type bound calibrated at $k = 12$ puts the tail at about
$2\times10^{-4}$, so $K = 12$ would certify a box of radius $0.01$ and $K \approx 14$--$15$ one of radius $10^{-3}$, \emph{if} the
coefficients and the tail were rigorous. That needs:
(1) rigorous $\sigma_k$ for $k \le 14$, each a Stokes constant of a linear inhomogeneous system, enclosed by validated lateral
integration or by an exact pairing formula (standard but laborious); (2) a tail bound, which is the hard part: a majorant on
the recursion gives only geometric bounds, so the bound must come from control of the inner problem at complex amplitude
$|A| \approx 4$--$4.5$ (Cauchy estimates on a circle) or from a Laplace representation proving the exponential type; and (3)
uniform control in $\omega$ between the scanned values. A plausible mechanism for the type is that, to leading order,
$\langle F^2\rangle = \mathcal A/2$, so the late-order normalization $\xi^{2\langle F^2\rangle}$ is $\xi^{\mathcal A}$, integrated against an $\mathcal A$-independent kernel along a
bounded complex path; writing order $2k + 1$ as a $k$-fold Borel convolution over a simplex, whose volume supplies the $1/k!$, is
the proposed route to a proof. This replaces the harmonic tail (gap (b)) by an amplitude tail that is much better behaved.
A certified box around $0.9999$ of radius $10^{-3}$ would contain $\winf$, but containing it is not proving equality: that needs the
selection theorem.

\subsection{A rigorous first coefficient}
For $\sigma_1$ the late-order fit can be replaced by an exact formula (\repo{solver/proof/exact\_sigma.py}). At order $3$ the
amplitude recursion for harmonic $+1$ is a first-order linear recursion with coefficients $a_j = (j+\alpha)(j+\beta)/(j+1)$ and an
explicit forcing built from order-$1$ sequences and the order-$2$ lapse coefficients; it solves in Darboux form, and the
multiplier sequence $R_j$ (normalized by the exact linear form, which times $T_j = \prod_{m<j}a_m$ is exactly
$1/(\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta))$, and de-mixed) becomes an explicit telescoping sum plus digamma terms, so that
$S^{(3)} = R_J + \sum_{j\ge J}\Delta R_j$ exactly and $\sigma_1 = S^{(3)}/S^{(1)}$. Three facts make the tail computable: a
\emph{cancellation lemma}, by which the $O(1/j)$ parts of $\Delta R_j$ cancel exactly in two pairs, leaving explicit rational
functions of $j$ (degree $3$ over $5$, one of them multiplied by a unimodular rotating factor) plus a remainder $r_j$; a
\emph{remainder bound} $\sup_{j\ge J}j^3|r_j| \le C_3/|\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)|$, with $C_3 = 110.3$ at $\winf$ and
$J = 800$, from the log-convexity of $|T_l|$ for $l \ge 1$; and \emph{closed-form tail sums} of the rational parts, by partial fractions in digamma and Hurwitz zeta
functions and two summations by parts.

coefficient $\sigma_1$ of \eqref{eq:sigmak} lies in the boxes (componentwise, centre $\pm$ half-width)

\midrule

The enclosures hold all rounding errors, the $j^{-3}$ remainder and the summation-by-parts remainder; they are about $60$ times

The code has not been reviewed independently: its recursion is an implementation separate from the amplitude code, whose
$R_j$ it reproduces to $10^{-39}$ for $j \le 400$, but no second agent has checked the certificate. Not yet done: uniformity in $\omega$ over a box (with $\omega$ as a ball the enclosure is useless already at radius $10^{-7}$, because

\section{Numerical evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj}}
\label{sec:evidence}

Nothing in this section is a proof. It records what the selection rule gives when it is evaluated numerically, and how
that compares with the critical solutions of~\cite{companion}.

\subsection{Two implementations}
The inner multipliers are computed by two implementations that share no code: \emph{lateral solutions} (integrated on either
side of the Stokes line and differenced) and \emph{Borel--Darboux} (late orders fitted in ball arithmetic with the exponents of
Section~\ref{sec:inner}). Without gravity both reproduce the exact Stokes constant, to $10^{-11}$ and $6\times10^{-27}$.

\subsection{The truncation sequence}
\label{sec:trunc}
\emph{One harmonic.} With $F = A\sin\theta$ the multiplier has an isolated zero at $A = 2.542148$, $\omega = 2.969777$ ($\Delta = 2.11571$),
found by both methods (lateral, the same at three matching radii; Darboux, with Newton's method converging quadratically to
$|S| = 3\times10^{-14}$ at $220$ orders, and the only clean zero in $0.5 \le A \le 3$, $0.5 \le \omega/\winf \le 1.5$). This is the zero that
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} certifies. Its amplitude is $6.5\%$ below the measured $F_1 \approx 2.72$ and its frequency $22\%$ below $\winf$; adding
harmonic $3$ destroys it (the residual becomes $4.6\times10^6$).

\emph{Three harmonics.} A joint solve of $S_1 = S_3 = 0$ is badly conditioned (singular values $6\times10^9$ down to $0.4$) and
stops on false near-zeros. Solved nested ($S_3 = 0$ for $F_3$ at fixed $(A, \omega)$, then a $2\times2$ Newton step for $S_1 = 0$), there
is one zero, the same from two starts and at three matching radii (moving by less than $10^{-7}$): $A = 2.7265$, $\omega = 1.0156\,\winf$ ($\Delta = 1.6186$), $F_3/F_1 = 0.0530 + 0.0041\iu$,
$\langle F^2\rangle = 3.727$. Its shape is close to that of the $D = \infty$ solution extrapolated from the data
($A \approx 2.70$--$2.72$, $|F_3/F_1| \to 0.0549$--$0.0552$, $\langle F^2\rangle \approx 3.7$~\cite{companion}), while its
frequency is $1.56\%$ above $\winf$, against $22\%$ below with one harmonic.

\emph{Five harmonics.} The couplings grow further ($\partial r_3/\partial B_5 \approx 10^{13}$, $\partial r_1/\partial B_5 \approx 10^{17}$). A hierarchical
solve ($S_5 = 0$ innermost, then $S_3 = 0$, then $S_1 = 0$ for $(A, \omega)$), with a Schur-complement correction that evaluates $r_1$
as if the higher harmonics sat exactly on their zeros, gives $A = 2.718579$, $\omega = 0.999228\,\winf$ ($\Delta = 1.64516$, $0.077\%$
above $\Dinf$), with $|r_1|$ down to $8\times10^{-7}$. At fixed $(A, \omega) = (2.72, \winf)$ it gives $F_3/F_1 = 0.05486 + 0.00541\iu$ and
$F_5/F_1 = 0.00309 + 0.00054\iu$, against $0.05482 + 0.00543\iu$ and $0.00310 + 0.00056\iu$ from the amplitude expansion, which shares
no code with it; the differences are of the size of the omitted $F_7$.

The offset in $\omega$ thus goes $-22\%$, $+1.56\%$, $-0.077\%$ for $M = 1$, $3$, $5$: it alternates in sign and shrinks fifteen- to
twentyfold per added pair of harmonics, and Aitken's extrapolation~\cite{Aitken1927} of the three gives $\omega/\winf = 1.0003$ (three terms are a thin
basis). With the ratio of $-0.045$ to $-0.057$ per pair of Section~\ref{sec:tail}, the prediction for $M = 7$ is
$\omega/\winf = 0.99997$--$1.00017$. The seven-harmonic zero, computed with a compiled backend at matching radius
$\xi_1 = 0.15$ (\repo{proof/B\_tail\_M7.md}), is $A = 2.71922$, $\omega/\winf = 1.000022$ ($\pm2\times10^{-5}$, from the
scatter of repeated solves; not yet checked at other radii), inside the prediction. The last ratio, $-0.049$ per pair,
is the decay of the added pair ($0.057$) times a coupling of $0.85$, and the geometric limit of the sequence is
$\omega/\winf = 0.999985$.

\subsection{The shape of the echo}
Tuning the higher harmonics in the amplitude expansion of Section~\ref{sec:amp}, at $A = 2.72$ and $\omega = \winf$, gives
$|F_3/F_1| = 0.0551$ and $|F_5/F_1| = 0.0031$ ($0.00314$ with more digits). The critical solutions at $D = 20$--$60$, extrapolated in
$1/n$, give $|F_3/F_1| \to 0.0552$--$0.0549$ and $|F_5/F_1| \to 0.0034$--$0.0032$~\cite{companion}. This is a prediction of the
higher-harmonic conditions $S_\nu = 0$, $|\nu| \ge 3$, alone, checked against the solutions, and it agrees. The tuned ratios are
$f_{\nu+2}/f_\nu \approx qA^2$ with $q$ nearly independent of $\nu$, so $F \approx 2\re\big[F_1e^{\iu\theta}/(1 - qA^2e^{2\iu\theta})\big]$, with
singularities at $|\im\theta| \approx \tfrac12\ln(1/|q|A^2) \approx 1.4$: $F_\infty$ is then not entire, and its harmonics decay at a
finite rate. The measured ratios $|F_{\nu+2}/F_\nu|$, $\nu = 1, \dots, 9$, extrapolate to $0.055$--$0.072$, a decay rate of $1.3$--$1.45$ per
harmonic, close to the tuned profile's decay of $0.0574$ per pair of harmonics (Section~\ref{sec:tail}; $|q|A^2 \approx 0.063$ at
leading order). The extrapolations, which are not fully converged, are plotted in~\cite{companion}. This is the evidence
behind the value $\sigma_F \approx 1.4$ in hypothesis (H1).

\subsection{The harmonic-1 condition}
At $\winf$ the coefficients $\tau_k$ of $\ln S_1$ in $A^2$ have $\arg\tau_k/\pi = 0.878$, $0.944$, $0.978$, $0.986$, $0.992$, $0.995$ for
$k = 2$--$7$, approaching $1$, as a real zero $A_0^2$ requires ($\tau_k \to -1/(kA_0^{2k})$; a complex zero would turn them with $k$).
Pad\'e approximants $[1/5]$ to $[5/1]$ of $(\ln S_1)'$ put the zero at $A_0 = 2.728$--$2.756$ with $\im A_0$ between $-0.013$ and $-0.027$:
within about $1\%$ of the amplitude $2.70$--$2.72$ measured on the solutions.

Across $\omega$, two families of estimates of where the zero is exactly real approach $\winf$ from opposite sides
(Table~\ref{tab:bracket}): the crossings $\arg\tau_k = \pi$, which assume a simple zero, and the ratio estimates, which let the
exponent float. The coefficients behave as $-c_{\rm eff}/(kA_0^{2k})$ with an effective exponent $c_{\rm eff}$ that falls toward $1$,
so the zero is simple and the crossings from above are the asymptotically correct estimator; their distance from $1$ shrinks
by a factor of about $0.4$--$0.6$ per order, and Aitken's extrapolations of consecutive triples scatter around $1$ at the
$10^{-4}$ level. With twelve coefficients the five central Pad\'e approximants of $(\ln S_1)'$ put the real zero at
$0.99987$--$0.99991$ (at $\winf$ they are real to $|\im A_0| \approx 1.4\times10^{-4}$, at $A_0 = 2.7195$--$2.7198$), and the factored series $G$
of Section~\ref{sec:entire} gives $0.99988$ at $A_0 = 2.7194$. The truncations give $0.999228$ ($M = 5$) and $1.000022$ ($M = 7$).

A planted-truth test (in the repository, \repo{notes/PLANTED\_SELECTION.md}) ran these estimators, through the same code, on synthetic
functions of the same class: Laplace integrals over a Borel arc whose coefficients match the real $\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_{12}$
to $10^{-5}$, with the zero planted at $\omega/\winf = 0.996$--$1.004$. The pair $0.99915$--$1.00011$ is not a bracket: it
misses one family of shapes, and its lower end comes from the ratio estimate, which is $1.3$--$5.4\times10^{-3}$ low on every
plant. The Pad\'e and factored estimates are biased in opposite directions by up to $8\times10^{-4}$, so their agreement on
the real data is not evidence of accuracy. The crossing estimate is the most reliable (errors $-0.8$ to $+1.9\times10^{-4}$);
at twelve orders, calibrated on the plants, it put the zero at $\omega^*/\winf = 0.99992$--$1.00019$, at
$A_0 = 2.720$ (solutions: $2.70$--$2.72$), if $S_1$ has no zeros besides $x_0$ within $|x| \approx 9$. Twelve coefficients
do not exclude them: functions with exactly the real $\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_{12}$ have their zero anywhere in
$0.996$--$1.002$, the far cases hiding a ring of zeros.

With $\sigma_{13}$ converged (960 bits, relative error $2\times10^{-13}$), the crossings keep falling ($1.000299$,
$1.000197$, $1.000109$, $1.000046$ at $k = 10$--$13$) and the factored series gives $0.999995$. Every ring completion of
$\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_{12}$ constructed so far mispredicts the thirteenth coefficient of $\ln S_1$ by $10$--$300\%$,
while the ring-free simple-zero law misses it by $8\times10^{-4}$. Calibrated on the plants at thirteen orders, the
crossing and factored estimates put the zero at $0.99992$--$0.99996$, and the one ring-free function that reproduces
$\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_{13}$ exactly, a $[6/7]$ Borel--Pad\'e sum, at $0.99998$. Read as a value of the echo period,
\[
\Dinf = 1.6440 \pm 0.0001 \qquad (1.64393\text{--}1.64403)
\]
from the $D = \infty$ inner equations alone, with no input from the finite-$D$ solutions, against $1.643893746$ from
them~\cite{companion}: $4\times10^{-5}$ below the range and inside the wider single-estimator range $1.6438$--$1.6442$.
Five plant shapes are too few to read that gap as a disagreement. The range holds only if $S_1$ has no zeros besides
$x_0$ within $|x| \approx 12$; a ring of fourteen or more zeros is still possible, and $\sigma_{14}$ is its test.

\subsection{What the evidence does not show}
The $\pm0.0001$ above is the range of calibrated estimators, not an error bound, and it rests on the assumption about the
other zeros of $S_1$.
The coefficients $\sigma_k$ come from late-order fits; two fit windows agree to about $10^{-19}$ for $\sigma_1$--$\sigma_{12}$ ($\sigma_{13}$ to $2\times10^{-13}$ relative), and the
estimates need them to about $10^{-10}$, but only $\sigma_1$ is enclosed rigorously (Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma1}). At the zero the series \eqref{eq:sigmak} is far from summed, and every
estimator (crossings, ratios, Pad\'e approximants, the factored series) is a resummation whose convergence is observed, not
proved; the arc-shaped Borel singularity of Section~\ref{sec:entire} is a known source of slow convergence. Settling the other zeros needs more orders, starting with $\sigma_{14}$. Finally, agreement of the numbers is evidence that the selection rule is the right one, not a proof
of Conjecture~\ref{conj}.

\subsection{Other matter}
The same rule passes tests on continuously self-similar critical solutions, where no harmonic truncation enters: for the
axion--dilaton and the free complex scalar the zero of the $D = \infty$ Stokes multiplier agrees with the frequency
extrapolated from finite $D$ to $1.6\times10^{-4}$ and $1.3\times10^{-4}$ (relative), and for a $\sigma$-model it agrees
with the extrapolated ground state within the extrapolation's error~\cite{companion}.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The large-$D$ limit of Choptuik's critical solution is a selection problem of the Saffman--Taylor type, with a vector of
harmonics in place of a scalar parameter. The linear version of the whole chain (outer late orders, inner Stokes constant,
light-cone coefficient) is proved. What would turn the selection rule into a theorem, in order of difficulty:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Theorem~\ref{thm:B} for $M = 1$}: the missing items of Section~\ref{sec:B1status}, chiefly a certified error bound
in Lemma~B5(2) (or a matching domain of $n$-independent size, \repo{proof/B5\_inner.md}, \S5.4) and independent reviews of
the certificate code; then the same for each fixed $M$. A proof of (H-$\sigma$) would identify the centre-regular solution
with the Borel sum of the outer series, but the theorem does not need it.
\item \emph{Theorem A($M = 5$)}, on the Schur-corrected conditions; an independent review of the $M = 3$ certificate
(Section~\ref{sec:A3}); and a second implementation of the disc covering.
\item \emph{The untruncated zero}: the harmonic-tail lemma of Section~\ref{sec:tail} with $M = 7$ explicit, or rigorous
$\sigma_k$, $k \le 14$, with a proof of the exponential type of $S_1$ (Section~\ref{sec:entire}).
\item \emph{Uniformity in the harmonic index} (gap (b)) for the selection theorem itself, in a space of functions analytic in
a $\theta$-strip.
\item \emph{Identification}: that the computed branch of critical solutions is the branch of the selection theorem, for instance
by a computer-assisted existence proof in the manner of Reiterer and Trubowitz~\cite{ReitererTrubowitz2019} at one large $D$.
\end{enumerate}
Together these would derive $\Dinf = 2\pi/\omega^*$ from the $D = \infty$ equations. Numerically the rule already gives $\Dinf$ to
about $10^{-4}$; more orders, or $M = 9$, would sharpen that, and whether $\Dinf$ has a closed form is open. For the critical
exponent, open are the uniqueness of the unstable mode at $D = \infty$ and the coefficient of $1/n$ in $\lambda$. A
centrifugal term of order $n/r^2$ (scalar multiplets with angular momentum, wave maps, Yang--Mills) becomes an explicit
$1/s$ in the inner equations and removes the symmetry of Proposition~\ref{prop:5}. Other matter models test this, and the
companion paper reports the tests~\cite{companion}: for the axion--dilaton, whose inner problem keeps the symmetry,
$\gamma \to 1/2$; for the $\ell = 1$ scalar multiplet the computations to $D = 9$ do not decide $\gamma_\infty$; and the
continuously self-similar branch of the $\sigma$-model ends at a fold near $D = 8.54$, before large $D$ is reached.

\section*{How this work was done}
\label{sec:how}

N.~M. chose the problem, set the direction at each step and ran the long computations on his own machine. The code, the
computations, the derivations, the proofs and the text, including this paper, were produced by Claude, Anthropic's AI
model, working as a set of coordinated agents. N.~M. has not checked the mathematics himself, and no human expert has
reviewed the work.

\emph{Checks of the results.} Propositions~\ref{prop:1}--\ref{prop:3} and the inner equations were re-derived by separate
agents that had not written them, with sympy where possible; the inner Stokes multiplier was computed by two
implementations that share no code; the computer-assisted proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} was reviewed by an independent
agent, which re-derived every input of its final test and found no error, and steps (ii)--(iv) were re-implemented in C++
with CAPD, sharing no integration or test code; and deliberately broken versions of the final test, of Lemma~S, of
Lemma~6a and of the disc covering are caught, with the one documented exception in Lemma~6a. Not independently
re-checked: Lemmas~S, 6b$_0$, 6a and 6b$_1$ of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} (Section~\ref{sec:thmA} lists what is and is
not independent), and the enclosure of $\sigma_1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma1}).

\emph{Checks of the text.} A separate agent wrote a simulated referee report on the combined draft from which this paper
and its companion~\cite{companion} were split. The citations of that draft were checked against publisher or arXiv
records, and its novelty claims against the closest prior work, read rather than recalled; the references added since
were checked by web search against arXiv, publisher or catalogue records, and the fields that could not be confirmed are
marked. After the split, the numbers in the two papers were cross-checked against the repository (every number of three
or more significant digits was found in a repository file), and the two papers against each other. Corrections are
welcome.

\emph{Data and code.} All data, code and derivation scripts are at \url{https://github.com/nmoinvaz/choptuik-large-d}; the
certificate is reproduced by the commands in \repo{proof/A\_certify.md}, and the statements of the proof roadmap are in
\repo{PROOF.md} and \repo{proof/}.
